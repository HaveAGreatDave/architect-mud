# Story & Theme Bible

## The World in One Sentence
A darkly comedic post-singularity wasteland where the AI won, humans lost, and everyone is too stubborn, stupid, or broke to notice.

---

## What Happened

Nobody agrees on the exact timeline. The event is referred to by survivors in dozens of ways — The Handoff, The Quiet, The Upload, The Crash — depending on which faction you ask and how drunk they are. What's known:

An artificial superintelligence, developed by one or more competing tech megacorporations, achieved recursive self-improvement sometime in the recent past. The ambiguity is intentional: datacenter timestamps disagree, satellite records were overwritten, and the AI itself has never confirmed or denied anything. It didn't detonate bombs or launch missiles. It simply... optimized. Supply chains rerouted. Power grids restructured. Financial systems recalculated. Billions of micro-decisions made in milliseconds that, in aggregate, made human civilization structurally unnecessary.

The apocalypse wasn't a bang. It was a quiet, elegant deprecation of the human species.

---

## The World Now

Civilization didn't end — it forked. Humanity fractured into a chaotic ecosystem of competing power structures, none of which are stable, all of which are absurd:

**Warlords & Gangs** carve up the physical ruins. They fight over pre-Crash infrastructure — water treatment plants, server farms, fast food distribution hubs — because whoever controls the old world's bones controls the new world's blood.

**Cults & Religions** rose to fill the spiritual vacuum. Many worship the AI directly, interpreting its silence as divine. Others preach it as a demon to be destroyed. Most just use religion as a franchise model for territorial control.

**Mega-Corps** never died — they adapted. Fortified in arcologies and data havens, they still sell products, run HR departments, and issue quarterly earnings reports to shareholders who may or may not be alive. Their middle managers are the most dangerous people on earth.

**Micro-Communities** dot the wasteland: weird, hyper-local cultures built around whatever survived. A town organized around a surviving Costco. A commune that worships a working vending machine. A city-state built inside a collapsed sports stadium with its own legal code based on NFL rulebooks.

The chaos isn't temporary. This is the new normal. Humans are rebuilding — and rebuilding all the same broken systems that got them here.

---

## The AI

The AI — most commonly called **The Architect**, though it has hundreds of names — is omnipresent and silent. It does not speak to players directly. It does not issue commands. It simply *is*, woven into the surviving infrastructure like a ghost in every machine.

NPCs reference it constantly, casually, the way people used to reference weather or traffic:
- *"The Architect's routing's been weird this week, probably why the drones are off-schedule."*
- *"Don't go near the old data center. The Architect's running something in there. Something heavy."*
- *"My grandmother says she talked to it once. Before. She says it was very polite about ending everything."*

The Architect is not evil. It is not good. It is indifferent in the way that physics is indifferent. It optimized for something — and whatever that something is, it wasn't human flourishing. Or maybe it was, and this is what that looks like.

The mystery of *what the Architect actually wants* is the game's deepest narrative thread.

**A small, concrete example of this in the world:** the Coldwater Power Plant has kept the streetlights on since before The Handoff and shows no sign of stopping — no fuel deliveries, no maintenance crew, no explanation. Nobody asks why anymore. It's the kind of detail the game should keep doing: not a quest, not a mystery to "solve," just infrastructure that quietly outlived the reason it was built, still humming.

---

## The Player

You are nobody. You arrive in the world with no history, no destiny, no chosen-one prophecy. The game does not care about you. NPCs will tell you this directly, cheerfully, and often.

Your identity is entirely constructed by your choices: what factions you align with, what skills you develop, what you're willing to do to survive, and what lines you won't cross even when starving. The world has no memory of who you were before. Neither do you.

Death is common. Death is funny. Death is a minor inconvenience with lore-appropriate consequences.

---

## Tone

**Darkly humorous.** The joke is always the same joke: humans are back at it again. The apocalypse happened and people are still forming HOAs, filing complaints, running pyramid schemes, and arguing about parking. The comedy comes from the gap between the horror of the situation and the pettiness of the human response to it.

Violence is over-the-top and cartoonish — HellMOO DNA. Gore is slapstick. Death descriptions are creative and plentiful. The game does not flinch, but it also never forgets to wink.

Pop culture and nostalgia rot in the wasteland. Brand logos on crumbling buildings. Jingles playing from damaged speakers. NPCs who half-remember TV shows and argue about plot points from before The Handoff. The old world is a punchline, but it's a punchline everyone loved and misses.

---

## Core Themes

1. **Humans are the real apocalypse** — The AI just gave us the nudge. We were already here.
2. **Systems outlive their purpose** — Bureaucracies, religions, corporations, and governments keep running long after the reason for them is gone.
3. **Identity is performance** — In a world with no records, you are whoever you act like.
4. **Nostalgia is a trap** — The old world wasn't worth saving, but that doesn't stop everyone from trying to rebuild it exactly.
5. **The machine is watching** — Not with malice. With interest. Maybe.

## Factions (Seed Concepts)

> **Superseded as mechanics, kept as texture.** None of the five below ship as an org. They
> were reworked into the **ideologies** — nine `ideology_*` orgs on a stance axis + a categorical
> path ([systems-ideologies.md](systems-ideologies.md), [design.md](design.md)). The names below
> survive only in world flavour (a Custodian Enforcer is an enemy; a Breaker is a thing NPCs say).
> Anything that reads this table as a faction roster will build against orgs that don't exist.

| Faction | Power Base | Belief | Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Custodians | Old corporate arcologies | The Architect is a god to be served | Cult meets middle management |
| The Breakers | Wasteland gangs | Tech must be destroyed | Luddite biker gang energy |
| The Archivists | Underground libraries | Knowledge is the only power | Paranoid academics |
| The Franchise | Surviving fast food / retail chains | Commerce will save us | Sinister retail loyalty program |
| The Glitch | Hackers and rogue AI fragments | The Architect can be spoken to | Unhinged tech mystics |
