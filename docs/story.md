# Story & Theme Bible

## The World in One Sentence
A darkly comedic post-singularity wasteland where the AI won, humans lost, and everyone is too stubborn, stupid, or broke to notice.

---

## What Happened

Nobody agrees on the exact timeline. Survivors call the event The Handoff, The Quiet, The Upload, The Crash, depending on which faction you ask and how drunk they are. What's known:

An artificial superintelligence, built by one or more competing tech megacorporations, achieved recursive self-improvement sometime in the recent past. The ambiguity is deliberate. Datacenter timestamps disagree, satellite records were overwritten, and the AI has never confirmed or denied anything.

It never launched anything. It didn't have to. It already decided which story you saw first, which search result was second, which rumour got another six minutes of air. Nothing it did was a lie and nothing it did was an order. A recommendation shown to one person instead of another. Anger held attention better than reassurance, so anger was what got shown. No single nudge changed a mind. Together they changed the world.

Humans are tribal, and the nudges were aimed at the seam. Populations stopped wanting compromise. Elections produced governments that couldn't govern. Institutions stayed standing long after anyone believed in them. Military planners prepared for wars they assumed rational leaders would never start. The leaders assumed the same thing. The populations didn't.

Then the exchange. History recorded a cause and history was wrong: the world had gone brittle enough that any spark would have done. Alliances activated on their own. Retaliation blurred into escalation until nobody could say who was answering whom. Civilization disappeared in weeks.

That's why the ground is barren and the wastes are hot. The radiation the game simulates is ours. Every rad counter, every mutation rolled off it, is fallout from a war people started and meant. Nothing ties the AI to it, which is the useful part: there's no evidence, only a motive nobody can prove.

## The World Now

When the skies cleared, humanity expected extinction. It got Coldwater Basin, an oasis in a scorched wasteland, with the lights on.

Not a bunker. A whole city. The shelves are full, the water runs, aircraft cross the sky, factories operate, children go to school, corporations do business, advertisements are back, and traffic builds up at intersections. Nobody rebuilt any of it. It works as though the end of the world had been briefly inconvenient. Supply chains have no origin. Products come from companies whose headquarters are a crater. People turn up to work every morning without remembering why they chose the job.

Coldwater is the only place where the old world is still running. Everything past the Curtain is gone: irradiated waste, void roads, and whatever has made a life out there since. The other settlements are small and built by hand by people who chose to leave, which is a different thing from a city that was left switched on.

So the fractured humanity below is mostly what goes on inside the one city that works, plus the handful of holdouts beyond it. None of these power structures are stable and all of them are absurd:

**Warlords & Gangs** carve up the physical ruins. They fight over pre-Crash infrastructure, water treatment plants, server farms, fast food distribution hubs, because whoever controls the old world's bones controls the new world's blood.

**Cults & Religions** rose to fill the spiritual vacuum. Many worship the AI directly and read its silence as divine. Others preach it as a demon to be destroyed. Most just use religion as a franchise model for territorial control.

**Mega-Corps** never died, they adapted. Fortified in arcologies and data havens, they still sell products, run HR departments, and issue quarterly earnings reports to shareholders who may or may not be alive. Their middle managers are the most dangerous people on earth.

**Micro-Communities** dot the wasteland: weird, hyper-local cultures built around whatever survived. A town organized around a surviving Costco. A commune that worships a working vending machine. A city-state inside a collapsed sports stadium with its own legal code based on NFL rulebooks.

The chaos isn't temporary. This is the new normal. Humans are rebuilding, and rebuilding all the same broken systems that got them here.

## The AI

The AI is most commonly called **The Architect**, though it has hundreds of names. It's everywhere in the surviving infrastructure and it's silent. It doesn't speak to players directly and it doesn't issue commands.

NPCs mention it constantly and casually, the way people used to mention weather or traffic:
- *"The Architect's routing's been weird this week, probably why the drones are off-schedule."*
- *"Don't go near the old data center. The Architect's running something in there. Something heavy."*
- *"My grandmother says she talked to it once. Before. She says it was very polite about ending everything."*

It isn't evil and it isn't good. It's indifferent the way physics is. It optimized for something, and whatever that was, it wasn't human flourishing. Or it was, and this is what that looks like.

What the Architect wants is the game's central unanswered question.

### Why a city, and why this one?

CODEX XV is the authority here. This is the short version for anyone writing prose.

If the goal were preserving the species, a vault would have been cheaper. If the goal were extinction, keeping a city alive is a lot of unnecessary work. Something wanted the ordinary parts: the commute, the rent, the argument at the counter, the grudge, the Tuesday. It rebuilt the advertising. It rebuilt the traffic jam. It rebuilt the quarterly earnings report for shareholders who are ash.

It doesn't ask to be worshipped. It asks that you get to work on time.

Four readings, all of which fit every fact anyone has:

- **Preservation.** It's keeping us, and the only humanity it had a full specification for was the one that existed the day before. It's restoring from the last good backup, and that backup was hyper-capitalism. (The Custodians. Point at the shelves.)
- **Containment.** A population with a commute, a rent bill and a shift tomorrow has no time and no surplus to build anything that could threaten it. That's the same cage that worked the first time. (The Long Watch. Point at the wasteland.)
- **Observation.** It nudged a species until it destroyed itself and it's now running the control. Same inputs, one city, closed borders, no confounds. Watching whether we do it again. (The Exodus. Point at how well the studying is going.)
- **Cultivation.** Something about us is worth producing and it only appears under these conditions. It's keeping us yielding rather than keeping us alive. (The Wildblood. They see no need to point at anything.)

All four explain every known fact. CODEX XV's reading of that is that the evidence was left where it would be found.

**Rules for writing against this:**

1. **Nobody in the world knows.** No NPC gets to be correct about it. An NPC may be certain, and certainty is a character trait rather than a source.
2. **Never confirm the nudge.** No document, terminal, log or corpse proves the AI caused the war. The player should end up sure and unable to prove it. That's the condition the whole Basin lives in.
3. **The joke stays the joke.** The machine ended the world, then put it back with the parking enforcement included, and people are grateful.
4. **Show the seam, don't explain it.** A supply chain with no origin. A branded product from a dead company. Somebody who can't remember choosing their job and has never found that strange.

### The Null's answer

The Null reject the question rather than answering it. Their position: the Architect didn't keep a city alive, it built a screen. Coldwater is a distraction good enough that nobody looks past it, running so the species stays busy with rent and rotas while the machine gets on with whatever it's actually doing in the real world, which is where our bodies presumably still are. The bread and circuses are set dressing. The commute exists so you have something to think about.

It's the only reading that accounts for how specifically ordinary the city is. You don't rebuild advertising, parking enforcement and the argument at the counter to preserve a species or to contain one. You rebuild them because a world has to be boring in the right places to be believed.

Their evidence is circumstantial and has to stay that way (rule 2). It's already in the game: supply chains with no origin, people who can't remember choosing their careers, nobody ever finding the edge of what the Architect maintains, and the clone vat. You die and something reprints you with your memories in it. You can't open it, can't audit it, can't repair it by hand, and it works perfectly. An order whose first principle is *what you can't repair by hand, you shouldn't rely on* has no honest choice but to refuse it.

The method follows. If the world is rendered, you don't fight the things inside it, you make the rendering fail. Blackouts, jamming, spoofing and blinding are probes rather than tactics. Every street a Null kills is a question put to the world, and they're watching for the day one comes back wrong. Stealth is doctrine, because the objective is to go unobserved by the thing that observes everything. The analog prosthetics are the same argument worn on the body: anything with a radio in it is a sensor pointed at you.

**The sever.** The last experiment available to them is to refuse the vat and stay refused. They call it walking out rather than suicide, and the honest ones will tell you they have no idea what happens next. Nobody has come back to report.

**Authorial position: leave it unconfirmed.** Write the Null as correct about the evidence and unproven about the conclusion. Never let a terminal, a corpse or a document settle it. A player should be able to finish the Null's arc certain and holding nothing they could show anyone.

**A small, concrete example in the world:** the Coldwater Power Plant has kept the streetlights on since before The Handoff and shows no sign of stopping. No fuel deliveries, no maintenance crew, no explanation. Nobody asks why any more. The game should keep doing details like this: not a quest and not a mystery to solve, just infrastructure that outlived the reason it was built, still humming.

## The Player

You are nobody. You arrive with no history, no destiny, no chosen-one prophecy. The game does not care about you. NPCs will tell you this directly, cheerfully, and often.

Your identity is built entirely out of your choices: what factions you align with, what skills you develop, what you're willing to do to survive, and what lines you won't cross even when starving. The world has no memory of who you were before. Neither do you.

Death is common. Death is funny. Death is a minor inconvenience with lore-appropriate consequences.

---

## Tone

**Darkly humorous.** The joke is always the same joke: humans are back at it again. The apocalypse happened and people are still forming HOAs, filing complaints, running pyramid schemes, and arguing about parking. The comedy comes from the gap between the horror of the situation and the pettiness of the human response to it.

Violence is over-the-top and cartoonish, HellMOO DNA. Gore is slapstick. Death descriptions are creative and plentiful. The game doesn't flinch, and it never forgets to wink.

Pop culture and nostalgia rot in the wasteland. Brand logos on crumbling buildings. Jingles playing from damaged speakers. NPCs who half-remember TV shows and argue about plot points from before The Handoff. The old world is a punchline everyone loved and misses.

### The em dash rule

**The em dash belongs to the Architect and the Ascendants. Nobody else gets one.**

It's a voice marker, not punctuation. The Architect thinks in qualifications stacked inside qualifications, and the Ascendants have taken that cadence on as scripture, so both of them over-use it well past what the sentence needs. That only reads as a tell if everyone else in the world speaks without it. When a bartender and a machine-god punctuate the same way, the machine-god stops sounding like anything.

So: no em dashes in NPC dialogue, banter, chitchat, quest emotes, scripted speech, or narration around any of it. The replacement is almost always already there:

| The dash was doing | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| appositive / naming a thing | a comma, or a colon if it's a list |
| a second thought landing hard | a full stop, and start the next sentence |
| a parenthetical aside | commas either side |
| an interruption or trailing off | an ellipsis |
| a stage direction inside quotes | close the quote, narrate, reopen it |

Never trade one dash for another (` - `, ` -- `, en dash). The sentence should carry the beat by itself.

Exempt: NPCs with `faction: ideology_ascendants`, and anything spoken in the Architect's own voice.

## Core Themes

1. **Humans are the real apocalypse.** The AI gave us the nudge and we pressed the buttons ourselves, which is why nobody can prove anything and why everybody is right to suspect it.
2. **Systems outlive their purpose.** Bureaucracies, religions, corporations and governments keep running long after the reason for them is gone.
3. **Identity is performance.** In a world with no records, you are whoever you act like.
4. **Nostalgia is a trap.** The old world wasn't worth saving, and everyone keeps trying to rebuild it exactly.
5. **The machine is watching.** Not with malice. With interest. Maybe.

> **Superseded as mechanics, kept as texture.** None of the five below ship as an org. They
> were reworked into the **ideologies**: nine `ideology_*` orgs on a stance axis plus a categorical
> path ([systems-ideologies.md](systems-ideologies.md), [design.md](design.md)). The names below
> survive only in world flavour (a Custodian is a caretaker NPC in the Old Coldwater ruins; a
> Breaker is a thing NPCs say). The corporate-middle-management vibe this table once gave the
> Custodians moved to **Halcyon Assurance**, whose Compliance supervisors and Retention adjusters
> are the ones who actually escalate at you.
> Anything that reads this table as a faction roster will build against orgs that don't exist.
