Eight eras

19 June · day one

Heckmud version 0.01

A room, a verb, and a Postgres connection string. Fifteen commits before midnight, four of them titled Update index.html, one of them titled "The best version yet" — of a thing that was nine hours old.

Three documents were written on day one and all three still govern the project: architecture.md (stack and schema), design.md (intent), and story.md (tone). The last one turned out to matter most; it is still the authority every line of player-facing prose is checked against.

20–25 June · week one

Two people, one file

The messy part. Merge, revert, merge back — CSS extracted into standalone files, reverted the same day, extracted again. Commit titles from this stretch include "I hate john akerson", "jackie is gone dave is BACK", and "Fixmeplz".

It also produced the single most consequential file in the repo: CLAUDE.md, born 20 June. Everything that followed — the rules about engine vs. content, no ORM, no startup migrations — was the argument this week made necessary.

The plugin system arrived almost immediately (weather, mutations, zone-validator on 20 June), and by the 25th there was a plugin-standard.md saying what a plugin owed the engine.

26 June – 2 July · the survival stack

The body keeps score

Combat gets body parts, typed soak, cooldowns. Corpses start leaving something behind. In one week the game acquires hunger, thirst, radiation, intoxication, drugs, jail, corps, crime, surveillance, and the broadcast system — twenty-plus plugins, most of which still ship unchanged in shape.

Hunger, thirst, and rads. Armor sums from every source. Corpses on the floor.

The important decision here wasn't any one system. It was that each of them became a plugin with its own regress suite rather than another branch inside a growing engine file.

3–8 July · CODEX

Git becomes the world

The cutover. World content stops living in a database somebody edits live and becomes one JSON file per entity under content/, with a push to main as the deploy. The old boot-time migrate() — which rewrote content on every restart and made dev unpredictable — is deleted outright.

The rule that came out of it

No startup migrations. Schema is idempotent DDL in one file; content is git; the deploy applies both. Nothing mutates the world because a process happened to boot.

7 July: CONTENT_READONLY goes on in production. The database stops being an author.

9–20 July · the sky

The ground was always data

Flight had existed as a banded model with minigame decks. It's abandoned for one continuous client-sim / server-reconcile loop, and the old code becomes unreachable legacy rather than a second supported path. Six aircraft, airfields, hazards, air combat, hangars, licences, insurance.

Stall horn. Redrock blur. The ground was always data — now it rushes up.

The flight sim turned out to be the project's most demanding consumer of world data, and it exposed things nothing else could: buildings without building_type rendered as flat grassland, which is how a whole category of authoring gap got found.

14–25 July · round trips

Latency lives in count, not cost

Prod Postgres is remote. A commit titled "less queries for the queeries" marks the week the team stopped optimising queries and started counting them.

  • Read tiers written into architecture.md — what lives in RAM, what's cached, what's cold.
  • Hot paths (per-move, per-swing, per-tick) may not add an awaited query at all.
  • Sync-by-contract helpers: getRelation, hygieneOf, wear().
  • A cache is only as safe as its write funnel — which is why furniture and npcs are deliberately not cached.
22 July – 7 August · the domestic turn

A pan is not a dish

The most surprising stretch. After a month of combat, crime and aircraft, the game spends three weeks on cooking, drinks, laundry, cleaning, hygiene, a library of public-domain books, a piano you play key by key on your own keyboard, and a stray cat.

Cooking alone produced three rules that read like the project's thesis: dry starch cooks in liquid so cook refuses a dry pan; a liquid answers every question about the pan and none about the dish; and an unmatched pan becomes an improvised dish named off a family table rather than "slop" — capped below the authored catalog, so masterful stays earned.

Why this mattered more than it looks

These systems have no combat value. They are the ones that made the world feel inhabited rather than traversed — and each was built by reusing a substrate that already existed (durability, relationships, senses, the gather hooks) rather than inventing a parallel one.

What the project learned the hard way

Most of the rules in CLAUDE.md are scar tissue. Each of these is a real outage or a real day lost.

The backtick

Client panels are giant HTML template literals. The house style writes prose comments inside them, and quotes verbs in backticks. A `horn` inside an HTML comment in cab-view.js ended the string mid-sentence and killed the entire client boot on production — chrome rendered, then no room, no vitals, no socket.

Fix: client:smoke now parses all 202 client files on every regress run. There is no build step, so before that, nothing between an editor and a player's browser ever parsed them.

The coffee roaster

One building model passed a palette key where the drawing helper wanted a style function. It froze the flight sim mid-frame — the first time anyone flew past that particular cafe. The only thing that ever ran a building model was a player happening to look at it.

Fix: shapes:smoke executes all ~83 building models against a DOM stub, night and day, both facings, in about a second.

The stale status line

On 27 July an audit reported five shipped systems as outstanding work. Every single error traced to a doc header that said "Not Yet Built" above a body full of ✅ markers.

Fix: docs:lint fails when a status header contradicts its own body. Compound status ("Phases 0–2 built; rest design") passes, because mixed status is the normal case.

The correction that cost two one-shots

CLAUDE.md claimed for weeks that the content deploy "can never touch existing rows". It can — the import is ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE SET <every file column>. Believing otherwise sent two unnecessary hand-run scripts at production before anyone read the importer.

Fix, 2 August: the paragraph was rewritten in place, with the date and the reason attached.

The through-line

Read the 69 system docs end to end and the same instinct shows up in every one, phrased differently:

  • Never store what you can derive. Mutation contributions are netted at read time. Heat decays lazily off a timestamp. The shopping list stores what you want, never what you have. The odometer is derived from position, because a self-reported distance lets a client weave and get paid for it.
  • One source of truth, or the bug is invisible. The stray cat hides by being in a real, exitless room — because a flags.hidden honoured by the room filter would need honouring by six other readers, and the first one anybody forgot is an invisible cat you can still stab.
  • Reuse the seam, don't build a second one. A natural weapon is a weaponStats object. Telekinesis fires existing verbs. A card is a snapshot, not a new subsystem. An augment is an inventory item first, which is how it got condition, trade and looting for free.
  • The refusal is the design. A list that doesn't partition usefully stays flat, and that's a success. Psionics below Seer may not name a mechanism. The search verb never pays out. Wings help you leave a fight, never survive one.

Where it stands

A world of roughly 15,000 zone files across four regions, 664 items, 215 NPCs, 1,240 pieces of furniture. 130 plugins, each with its own regression suite, sweeping their manifests against the live registries on every run. No build step, no ORM, no framework — vanilla JS in the browser, raw WebSockets, one query() helper on the server.

Still design rather than built: the Wildlands systems on top of their map, corp espionage and NPC corp AI, the unlicensed Promethean augment path, most of psionics past telekinesis, and the unbuilt half of the trucking road — the gearbox, the trailer, the weigh station that detects weight and not contraband.
