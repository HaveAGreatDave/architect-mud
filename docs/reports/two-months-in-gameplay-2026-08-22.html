The number that surprised us

666 distinct verbs — 87 from the engine, 598 declared across 133 plugin manifests, with 19 shared between them where a plugin deliberately overrides a builtin. The engine is now less than an eighth of the command surface. Whatever else is true, the engine/plugin boundary is real rather than aspirational: you could delete the game and keep the machine.

II. Three phases, and what each one was for

The commit graph tells a misleading story on its own — August looks like a slowdown. It isn't. It's the month the work stopped being new mechanisms and started being new answers wired into mechanisms that already existed, which is cheaper in lines and more expensive in thinking.

June1,014
July1,006
August (22d)366

Commits per month. August is three weeks, not four — but the rate is genuinely down about a third, and it should be.

Phase I — June: is there a place?

Survival, economy, world simulation, posture, ATMs, broadcast. Three system docs date from June and they are the three the game cannot exist without: systems-world, systems-survival, systems-economy. The output of June was a city with a clock, a weather roll, a power grid, five meters that deplete, and somewhere to spend money.

Phase II — July: is there anything to do in it?

Thirty-one system docs appear in July. Crime and policing, jail, surveillance, corporations, ideologies, scavenging, fishing, mining, swimming, flight, the casino, weather that kills you by name, void travel between regions, dreams, stealth, senses, hygiene, durability, NPC relationships, the library, drinks, procedural audio. This is the month the game stopped being a place and became a set of loops — and, critically, the month the loops started sharing substrate rather than each carrying their own copy of everything.

Phase III — August: is there anyone to be?

The five disciplines land in a single week — mutations, augments, psionics, mastery, nullcraft — and with them the first real answer to "what kind of character am I". Alongside: chess, playable instruments, bounties on players' heads, long-haul trucking, a stray cat, the display-mode ladder, the accessibility surface, and client automation. Fourteen docs, 366 commits.

Why the rate fell and that's the good news

August's biggest system, nullcraft, integrates with the rest of the game through one line in deviceStatus() and one filter in getAugments(). Psionics reads a decaying event buffer that no other system had to be modified to feed. Mastery grants no permanent passive at all. When new gameplay costs a hundred lines instead of four thousand, the commit count drops and the game gets deeper faster. That's the shape you want at month two.

III. The first hour

A new player opens a tab, registers, and gets a thirty-second cold open — a cinematic that plays before the prologue is allowed to speak. The prologue holds every line of arrival prose until the client echoes introdone, so the backstory never talks over itself.

Then you wake in a cloning vat. The tablet is issued there, not in the corridor before it, and the smartbar offers its own twenty-five-second tutorial as a dismissible chip rather than a modal. The CODEX app — the game's backstory as a readable system — opens sealed, and unlocks a chapter at a time as NPCs explain things to you. A sealed chapter's prose never leaves the server.

What the first hour actually teaches, in order: move (the room pane is clickable; every exit, NPC, item and enemy is a link that types the command for you), look and examine, take and wear, and then the meters start to bite. New verbs are introduced with a shimmer on their first mention and a ripple on the room-pane link that would run them — a house convention, applied everywhere.

By the end of the first hour a player who follows the nudges has: a tablet whose home screen seeds sixteen apps in deliberate reading order, a job board posting, a vendor who remembers them, somewhere warm, and a reason to be worried about at least two of hunger, thirst, radiation, body temperature and sanity.

Bare call and summon belong to poker, which is why calling the stray cat is an input matcher rather than a command and every pattern but pspsps carries her name. Speedwalk (3n2e) requires a digit, because "only direction letters" matches use, sew, wed and dune. The verb table is a shared resource and it has started to behave like one.

V. The body you are playing

The character sheet is 29 skills across five categories, ranked 0–10, raised by use rather than by spend. Sitting underneath it are the meters — HP, sanity, hunger, thirst, radiation, stamina, body temperature, hygiene — and 22 status effects that hang off them. None of that is remarkable; MUDs have had it for thirty years. What's new this month is what you can do to the body itself.

Five disciplines, five prices

The elegant part, mechanically, is that mastery's purity cap is what makes the other four cost anything. Chrome and mutation used to be free upside. They aren't now, because there is exactly one build in the game they lock you out of — and it is the build that looks, on inspection, like nothing at all.

Mastery is also the only progression in the game that runs the opposite way to everything else: every other build gets worse over a long fight, and this one gets better, because read accumulates against an enemy's template id and learns from misses as well as hits.

The design rule shared by all five

Nothing is baked into players.stat_*. Every contribution is derived at read time and netted by the same readers that already net gear and status effects. A mutation at expression 100 reproduces its authored value exactly; an augment defaults to calibration 100 for the identical reason. That invariant is what made both migrations net-zero for existing characters — and it is what makes partial treatment possible at all.

What you can be done to

Radiation deliberately cannot roll a mutation above expression 84, because a legendary mutation you could pick up by standing in a hot room would delete the reason to walk south and find the Wildblood. The mutagen flask is on a vendor's shelf — behind a trust gate that nothing raises except finishing the Quickening arc, so for anyone who hasn't been through the Pool it does not exist.

Medically there are three tiers at three prices: diagnose gives you a name and deliberately nothing else (an unseen mutation reads as "Something unnamed" until a physician names it — a diagnostic that also helped would make not-knowing a penalty rather than an information state); suppress holds it down temporarily; treat permanently reduces it, expensively, and buys your social standing back.

VI. Combat, and dying

A swing is 3,500 ms at base, shifted by whichever of five stances you're locked into (berserk, aggressive, normal, cautious, pacifist), floored at 1,000 ms. That is HellMOO pacing, not an action game: you have time to read the room, and the decisions are made before the swing rather than during it.

Every hit rolls a body part off a weight table — torso 40, arms 12 each, legs 11 each, head 10, feet 4 — and the head crit converts into a stun rather than a damage spike. Soak is typed. Injuries hang off damage events and have to be mended. Gear wears out on use rather than on the clock, with capacity derived from item value so nothing needs authoring, and at zero condition an ordinary item is destroyed outright.

Death is a cloning vat: skills retained, body reset, gear on the floor where you fell. Installed chrome corrupts on death unless the death was claimed by a cortical backup — an ordering constraint that matters because getting it backwards deletes the rows a restore is about to write back.

The best small decision in the combat model

A knockout is always something somebody chose. There is no random KO mid-fight, because auto-attack would finish an unconscious body a tick later and every fight would become ambiguous about whether you meant to kill. So the cosh is a deliberate 2★ camera-only crime, finishing someone who is already down is charged as execution at 5★, and police at 4★ or above now take you alive rather than killing you — closing the one route that used to skip jail entirely.

Combat also, quietly, has no parallel implementations. A mutant's claw is a weaponStats object — the same plain object a pipe produces, handed to the same attack function — so soak, crits, injury and body parts treat a clawed hand identically, and picking up a pipe means you use the pipe. Psionic telekinesis fires existing verbs at a distance rather than reimplementing them.

Why the bounty system is the sharpest economic design in the game

Because the head is an object, the theft, the ambush at the board, and the third party who was watching your fight for free all come out of the inventory system rather than being written. The sheet names nobody — a bounty you had to sign would be a declaration of war nobody would post — and the target pays to un-mask it. Escrow is the number printed on the sheet, so a poster can never advertise more than the hunter is paid.

VIII. The quiet game

Roughly half the verb surface has nothing to do with fighting or earning. This is the part of the game that surprised us most in the counting, and it is the part most likely to be why somebody stays.

The kitchen

61 authored dishes matched on ingredient signature rather than name — which is why renaming a saved recipe is free, why one combination can only be saved once, and why seasoning isn't part of the identity. Dry starch cooks in liquid rather than in heat, so cook refuses a dry pan of pasta and you fill the pan at a water source. A recipe you invent is a tradeable card or is taught face-to-face with recipe teach, and travels with your name on it either way. The shopping list stores what you want, never what you have, so it can't go stale.

The bar

22 drinks, mixed onto the vessel's own data rather than into a new item row — because the whole point of a mug is that you keep it. Alcohol is derived from ABV times pours and lands on the ordinary intoxication path, so a mixed drink and a bottled one get you drunk identically. Water and ice fill a glass without scoring it.

The piano

Furniture flagged as an instrument is sat down at with play and then played key by key on your own keyboard, and the room hears it. Notes ride a thin websocket route rather than the command dispatcher, because noodling is 8–12 inputs a second. Your own note sounds in the same tick as the keydown and tells the server after — a round trip between pressing a key and hearing it is the difference between an instrument and a website. It is the only thing in the game with no skill roll at all: the skill is your hands.

The board

Chess with full legal rules, perft-verified, on a two-seat table rendered server-side as an isometric neon set — the pieces counter-rotate so they stand up off the tilted plane; the shadow deliberately doesn't. Selection is two-step and server-side, so the client never computes legality. It shares its seat/spectator/host-NPC machinery with poker.

The rest of it

  • 13 books — a real reader with page and chapter navigation, RP narration with the spoken line highlighted, minimize-and-keep-reading, and a tap-to-gloss vocabulary layer over prose that is never rewritten. The bar for inclusion is US public domain.
  • Trading cards — mint yourself at a terminal, or open a foil sleeve from a vending machine. Buying and opening are two separate acts and the roll happens at openpack, so an unopened sleeve is untradeable-with-known-contents.
  • Television — 3 channels, 35 authored broadcasts, five live-assembled show modes, two sports leagues with narrated play, camera feeds, cassettes you load and eject, and a pirate verb for when you'd rather be on it.
  • A cat. Cathode is a stray with a bionic paw. Petting pays sanity on a six-hour cooldown and still works inside it, because you never punish a player for petting a cat. Kill her and the game never tells you why she stopped coming.
  • Housekeeping — hygiene, laundry, cleaning, mopping. A room you own keeps its mess for a full rent cycle while unowned space is swept nightly, which is the entire reason a mop is worth carrying.

The detail that says the most about the road

The highway signs itself, in miles, off one shared conversion used by the board, the route verb and the GPS — because a board saying 240 while the dash says 720 means nobody can budget a tank. A sign is snapped to a single tile at build time (a tolerance band renders a post as four identical boards in a row), the junction board looks 210 tiles past the fork because at ordinary look distance the limbs haven't separated yet, and passing a board is a swept range rather than a proximity test, because the cab reconciles four times a second and a text tick covers a slab of road.

X. Crime, and the law

Crime here is witnessed, not detected. 53 security devices sit in the world with status flags — jammed, spoofed, hijacked, looping, blinded — that predate the Null discipline by months and were promoted into it rather than duplicated. SPECTER lets players run their own spy networks: plant a device, retrieve it, sweep a room, pull a feed, record and clip it, or smash somebody else's.

The star ladder is priced by act rather than by outcome: a cosh is 2★ and camera-only; finishing someone already unconscious is execution at 5★. Get taken and jail is a real place — gear confiscation into an evidence locker, a hackable cell door, a sentence you can serve or escape, and a respawn zone seam that puts you in Holding rather than the vat.

Also available, for the enterprising: steal, peddle, pinch, burglary through hololocks with alarms, safecracking, till-cracking, graffiti, feed hijacking, broadcast piracy, and bribing your way off the wanted list.

XI. The city with nobody in it

Nobody logged in. The streetlights still came on at dusk, and rent came due. — on the one-minute driver tick, which advances the clock by scaled game-minutes and fires the 30-game-minute and game-day boundaries it crossed, so the calendar can never desync from a sped-up day

This is the section that separates a world from a menu, so it gets the specifics.

  • One clock, one driver. A single 1m tick advances game time at a configurable scale and fires everything downstream on game-minute boundaries. Day 07:00–17:00, dusk 17:00–20:00, night 20:00–05:00, dawn 05:00–07:00. Temperature swings on a cosine peaking +5°C at 14:00 and troughing −12°C at 02:00.
  • Weather is a field, not a flag. Drifting cloud, precipitation and storm cells over the outdoor map, fully re-derivable from the date plus tomorrow's forecast — no table, no per-tick writes. Cells drift along one seeded prevailing wind and torus-wrap. A cell pulls temperature down by up to 4°C at its core.
  • There is no free safe haven. A powered interior heads for 20°C at 2.0°C/min. An unpowered one bleeds toward outdoor temperature proportionally to the gap — a mild outage stays survivable, and a −30°C snap drops an interior to 10°C in about 23 minutes and to 0°C in about 51. A truck cab reaches its setpoint in a minute and loses it twenty times faster than a building, which is what makes a breakdown in a cold snap a problem rather than a wait.
  • The grid is real. 168 generators. Storms fault them, brownouts redistribute every five minutes but only when something is actually overloaded, and streetlights reconcile map-wide whenever ambient visibility drops below 0.35.
  • People have lives. All 243 NPCs have a home zone; 191 have a work zone. 85 ambient routines, 65 residences, 182 banter threads. NPCs commute, sleep, change clothes, and remember you: familiarity and warmth on a six-rung ladder, hydrated once at login and read from memory thereafter — zero runtime queries, by contract.
  • Rent falls due on the game calendar, not a real-world timer, so at 3× speed it lands roughly every two and a third real days.

Enemies spawn on a 10-second sweep against 328 authored spawn points. Ambient events fire every 45 seconds with a five-message recent window to avoid immediate repeats. Combat, and only combat, stays on real-world time.

XII. The surface you play it through

182,749 lines — the client is the single largest body of code in the repo, and it is vanilla JavaScript with no build step. What that buys a player:

  • A three-rung display ladder — visual, textgames, log — covering every system that has both a graphical presentation and a written one. The split is decided by one question: if I delete this surface, is the player stuck? Surfaces you act through degrade to text minigames; surfaces you only read degrade to log lines.
  • Client automation that is genuinely a language. Triggers, timers, aliases, macro variables, and macros that call macros with arguments and return values. Conditions are real expressions rather than a regex, which is what finally allowed if $hp_pct < 30 and has bandage. State triggers ride the vitals funnel and are edge-triggered, because level-triggering means drinking your entire inventory in one fight. A runaway budget disables rather than throttles, because a throttled runaway spams forever with nothing to explain it.
  • Output routing and split panes — a route is a trigger whose action is "put it over there", so it reuses that grammar exactly, and panes exist because a rule names one.
  • Accessibility as a first-class surface. One options table rendered by two surfaces — a tablet page and an accessibility verb with no tablet gate, because the light switch cannot be inside the dark room. Voice input is off by default; a player who never asks for a microphone is never asked for one. Read Aloud is off by default too, because the log is already a live region and on-by-default would speak every line twice in two voices.
  • Procedural audio — sound generated from action plus material plus intensity rather than per-thing assets, with 171 instrument presets, 195 samples and 26 effects. Footsteps are voiced by the ground you just stepped onto, alternate feet (random jitter reads as noise; an alternating pair reads as walking), and take wetness from the live precipitation as a delta.

The nuance the raw ratio hides

Measured against the whole map, population density looks like it collapsed. Measured against authored rooms, there are 2.6 hand-written rooms per NPC — the city is properly inhabited. What tripled is deliberately empty ground, and two systems shipped this month whose entire job is to make crossing empty ground a thing you do rather than a thing you endure. The honest statement is not "the world is too thin"; it is "the world now has two densities, and only one of them is finished."

The quest count is the real gap

77 quests. 132 objectives. Of those, 62 are hand-authored standard quests and 15 are flight templates; 44 are repeatable. Against 133 plugins and 666 verbs, that is the shortfall — the game has an enormous number of things you can do and a small number of reasons anybody tells you to. Job-board rotation papers over this for the first few hours and stops helping after that.

Coverage and the manual step

  • 13 of 133 plugins have no regress suite (90% covered, up from 83% in July). The ones that matter are the ones players touch constantly: scavenging, food, lighting, interactions, butchering, container.
  • Prod one-shots are still manual. CI never runs the seed-runtime pass, so lights, ATM units, vendor stock and NPC placements need a hand-run after a deploy. A deploy that isn't finished until a human remembers something isn't a deploy. This was flagged in July and is still true.
  • Only 16 NPCs carry inline banter against 182 authored threads — the threads exist, the cast wired to them doesn't yet.

Named design-only work

Explicitly unbuilt and documented as such: corp espionage and NPC corp AI; the Wildlands perimeter plugin and its faction NPCs (the map and wall are built content); boat boarding and underwater content for swimming; the helm's tablet mount; psionics beyond phase 1 — telepathy, precognition, biokinesis, dreamwalking, compulsion; nullcraft's network ladder; trucking's gearbox, trailer articulation, the yard and the weigh station; the card portrait renderer. That is a healthy backlog: every item on it is an extension of something shipped, not a foundation still missing.

XIV. The verdict

Two months in, Architect is a game you could put in front of somebody today and lose them for an evening — and the evening would probably not involve combat. They would end up cooking something, or flying badly, or sitting at a piano, or driving a truck three hundred miles to find out what a sign said.

The engineering position is strong and is not the interesting part. What's interesting is that the gameplay position matured in a specific direction this month: from adding mechanisms to adding answers. The five disciplines don't add systems so much as they finally make the four factions mean something at the keyboard, and they do it by asking questions of substrate that was already there. That is the pattern to keep.

The one thing that would change the game most is not on any system roadmap. It is ninety more quests — or whatever the equivalent is: authored reasons to walk into the 666 verbs. The machinery to deliver them shipped weeks ago; VINE authors them as a prerequisite DAG with a visual editor. The world is built, the loops are built, the body is built, and the city keeps its own time whether anyone logs in.

What it needs now is somebody in it with somewhere to be.
