What Architect is

Architect is a text MUD first: real time, brutal and funny, set after a singularity in a flooded basin city called Coldwater, with four ideological orders pulling at it and the Wildblood in the Under who were there first. You can play the whole thing by typing.

What's unusual is that every system the text describes is also something you can see, hear and touch if you want to. The room you're standing in can be walked through in 3D. The street outside is drawn by GLASS. The radio host is speaking with a synthesized voice. The fish on your line is worked out from the place and the clock, the same on server and client. The text stays the truth; the rest are views of it.

How it fits together

ArchitectThe game: Coldwater, the Basin, the orders, the prose. All content as one JSON file per entity, deployed by git.
ROOMWalking room to room, and building to street, on foot in 3D.
GLASSThe city, aircraft and streets in 3D, with GLASS Lite baked for phones.
SIREN · ORACLESynthesized sound and speech: no recorded VO, a 28k-word lexicon, FM cues, a tracker.
THOMASThe engine: commands, SIFT/FATE targeting, the event bus, plugin loader, CODEX pipeline, dev panel.
PostgresWorld state on Neon, with read tiers that keep the hot path off the network.

The pattern that keeps repeating: one shared pure function (fish, birds, cats, the Coliseum plan, a person's look) read by server, text, map and 3D alike. That's why so many views can exist without drifting apart.

Accomplishments worth naming

A city you can fly over and walk into

GLASS draws Coldwater from 224 models; ROOM walks you through the door of Screw It and up the stairs. The Building Stage joins the two.

A voice from nothing

ORACLE speaks broadcasts, the Architect and books with a formant synth and RP/GA accents. No audio files.

Two sports on the air

The CPhL with an NHL '96-style FM soundset, and Deadball at the Hughson Coliseum, a 25-tile deco bowl whose roof slides with the weather.

Chem Stage

Drug cooking as pure-sim chains in 3D, with concealed labs behind false walls. The picture decides nothing; the server does.

Scrappy

A cat with 51 clips, GPU skinning, real recordings and a day worked out from his id and the clock. The bar every pet must meet.

Git as the content writer

CODEX: a push to main backs up prod, applies schema, imports 40k+ content files, gated by regress.

Seats with real lettering

The Drake cockpit set the bar; the Merlin VTOL, trucks and skiff followed.

Accessibility from the start

Text, textgames and log rungs, voice input, Read Aloud. Phone layout with GLASS Lite.

Will it become something worthwhile?

Yes, I think so, with one condition. The hard part of a game like this is usually the stuff you've already done: a world with a voice, a platform that doesn't collapse under its own weight, and the discipline to keep it shippable. Five months in, Architect has more working systems than many MUDs reach in years, and the "one pure function, many views" pattern means each new view gets cheaper rather than harder.

The condition is players. Right now the project is graded against its own standards, and those are high. The next stretch should be graded against a stranger's first evening: do they understand where they are, find something to do in ten minutes, and want to come back? That's a narrower target than anything on the roadmap, and it's the one that turns an impressive engine into a game people talk about.

Suggested next five months, in order:

  • Pick one path through Coldwater for a new player and polish it to the Scrappy standard.
  • Finish phases rather than start them; close "rest is design" lines.
  • Get ten outside players in, watch them, and fix what they trip on.
  • Give main a clean-tree workflow so people and sessions stop costing each other work.

Happy five months. It's a good start.
