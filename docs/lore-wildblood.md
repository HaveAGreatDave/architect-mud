# The Wildblood

**Status: LORE (canon).** This is not a systems doc and describes nothing that is
built. It is the source of truth for what the Wildblood believe and do, and any
prose that contradicts it is wrong rather than interestingly different. The built
half lives in [systems-mutations.md](systems-mutations.md) (the mutagen, the
Quickening, the flask) and [proposals/scarletwastes.md](proposals/scarletwastes.md)
(the Thornwarren, the town behind the thorn wall).

⚠ **The Under is theirs and was theirs first.** This is the fact most likely to be
contradicted by accident, because the obvious assumption is that mutants come up
from the south. Some do. But the Wildblood were in the tunnels before the Long
Watch put a door on one, and a Watch character who talks about them as arrivals
is wrong.

---

## What they believe

Humanity was never meant to remain human.

They call ordinary human flesh **the First Shape** — the body given to mankind
before the world was broken. The First Shape is not sacred to them. It is an
evolutionary mistake: soft, fragile, and dependent on machines, walls, medicine,
electricity and the great artificial systems that collapsed.

The world is still changing. The radiation in the wastes, the growths beneath the
earth, the mutations appearing in children, the things that live where people no
longer do — these are not diseases.

They are **instructions**.

## Blood

Wildblood mutation is deliberately induced with a substance they call **blood**: a
crude mutagenic compound of irradiated biological material, mutant tissue,
contaminated medicines, fungal cultures, and whatever else has been found to
provoke the body's latent capacity for change.

Every tribe has its own recipes. Every elder has their own secrets. Nobody
outside the Wildblood knows exactly what goes into it, and perhaps nobody inside
does either.

They do not treat blood as medicine. They treat it as communion.

An initiate **chooses** a change they want — a stronger arm, heightened senses,
hardened skin, altered eyes, additional organs, claws, gills, extraordinary
musculature — and undergoes a ritual meant to make that change possible.

⚠ **The choosing is the part outsiders do not know**, and it is what makes the
outcomes legible:

| outcome | their word | |
|---|---|---|
| the change they asked for | **a Gift** | |
| something else entirely | **a Lesson** | |
| death | **a Refusal** | the body has told the truth |

A Refusal is not a failure. All three outcomes are meaningful, and a mutation
that leaves the initiate *weaker* may be celebrated more than one that does not,
because deliberately choosing the unknown is the greatest proof a person is free.

That table is the old way: blood drunk in the dark with nothing but the body to
decide. It's still how the tribes in the Under do it. In the Thornwarren the
machines under the Fleshery decide instead, and a ritual there gives the change
that was asked for (see [The machines](#the-machines)).

## The ritual

It begins with fasting — a day or more without ordinary food. The initiate is
washed, painted with ash and blood, and stripped. The tribe gathers and tells
stories of their ancestors: the first mutants, the survivors of the wastes, the
people who crawled out of burning cities and refused to become human again.

Then the drinking, while the others chant. The words vary between tribes; the
oldest form is said to be:

> **TAKE WHAT YOU ARE.**
> **BREAK WHAT YOU WERE.**
> **BECOME WHAT COMES NEXT.**

Then the initiate is taken into the dark, and this part is not incidental. A
mutation must not happen in comfort — the body has to be frightened, confused,
exhausted, driven to the edge of itself. Some rituals happen in the
Scarletwastes; others in abandoned hospitals, sewer chambers, flooded basements,
or the Under. The initiate is left alone for hours, sometimes days.

When they return, the tribe examines them. Not for beauty and not for health.
For **change**. A new tooth is celebrated. A second pupil is celebrated. A
useless deformity is celebrated.

## The machines

⚠ **Under the Fleshery the Wildblood keep computers and clone vats.** The bale,
clay and fire above are how they choose to live. They aren't all they have.

The machines came out of the Under, from the laboratories and hospitals the
city forgot, and were carried east piece by piece when the Thornwarren was
built. They run off a rotgas generator below ground, and nothing of them shows
in the town: no screen, no lamp, no cable above the floor. Fire is still the
only light anybody outside sees.

What they do:

- **They make the choosing real.** The machines read the initiate's body, and
  the vats grow the change that was asked for and set it in. In the Thornwarren,
  "taken into the dark" means down the stair under the Fleshery. A ritual there
  ends in a Gift.
- **They keep a body's changes on record.** Every member's shape is held, so a
  change made once is kept. The same machines can grow a phase further on a
  change somebody already carries.
- **They're mended by hand.** Every part has been opened, rebuilt and labelled
  by the person who looks after it. That's the difference they'd point to between
  their vats and the city's, which nobody can open.

This doesn't contradict what they believe. The First Shape is a mistake because
it needs machines to stay alive. A machine used to leave the First Shape is a
tool. They won't use one for anything a body can be made to do instead, and they
see no reason to explain that to anyone.

⚠ **Outsiders never see the machines, and nobody inside mentions them.** It's
the same rule as the choosing: the part outsiders don't know. Initiates go down
the stair and come back up changed, and the town treats that as ordinary.
Nothing in the city, the CODEX or a non-Wildblood's dialogue may describe them.

Some of the old ones go into the dark the old way anyway, with a flask and no
machine, because a Lesson is the braver story. The Kept is full of the ones it
went wrong for, from before the machines worked and since.

## The flesh rites

Some tribes burn their dead. Some bury them. Some leave them in the wastes. And
some **eat them** — a small portion, taken by members of the tribe.

The explanation is not appetite. They believe leaving a body intact is a kind of
imprisonment: the dead body belongs to the old world and the living body to the
new one, and nothing belonging to a Wildblood should remain entirely outside the
tribe after death. They call it **Taking the Blood Home**.

The Long Watch calls it cannibalism. The Wildblood call it inheritance.

There are also stories of enemies being eaten. The Wildblood insist those are
mostly told to frighten outsiders; the Long Watch insists they are told to
conceal the truth.

⚠ **Nobody has ever settled it, and nothing in the game may settle it.** The
Wildblood seem perfectly happy with that, which is the point — the same rule the
Long Watch material already runs on (see the "nothing is ever confirmed"
invariant in Teague's tree).

## Why there is a town, and why there are still people in the tunnels

⚠ **They did not choose the Under first. They were pushed into it.** This is the
causal spine and getting it backwards makes the whole faction read as monsters
who happen to live underground.

Mutants were in the city. The city stopped having anywhere for them — no room on
the Row, no work, Halcyon picking them up, and the Long Watch moving them on out
of the tunnels they had ended up in. So they went down, and then further down,
and then east.

**The Thornwarren is what the ones with anything left did about it.** A walled
town out in the redrock, built by people who were finished with being moved on,
somewhere nobody could move them on from again. It is a dignity project, which is
why the terror is all on the approach and the inside is domestic — see
[proposals/scarletwastes.md](proposals/scarletwastes.md).

**What stayed in the Under is what could not make that walk, or would not** — and
that includes the ones who have gone furthest into the changes, the feral end of
it. So the two populations are not two tribes with different opinions. They are
the same people sorted by whether they got out.

⚠ Which means a Long Watch character who moves families east is not neutral in
any of this. Nothing in the game should say so on their behalf; it is there for
the player to notice.

## The column

The town's road traffic is three rigs that run together: the Howl, the
hound-runner and the Slake cart. They run out of the Deadleg yard, up to the
North Gate and round to the Slake for water. What's built is in
[proposals/wildblood-column.md](proposals/wildblood-column.md).

- **Rotgas.** Their fuel is a biofuel they brew from the same fungal cultures as
  the blood, carried in skin bladders on the cab roofs and burned through open
  stacks. It's the loudest thing on the road, and the noise is most of the
  reputation. It costs them nothing.
- **It's a shift, not a war party.** The column is staffed off the same board as
  the North Gate mask rack: names against hours, chalked up, with an empty row
  for a recruit. The masks are lined with quilted rag so they don't chafe.
- **The bodywork is the city's.** The Howl carries a Mule freighter's fuselage
  off a wreck, with its Halcyon stripe left on and crossed out in ash. Halcyon
  blue only ever appears on a Wildblood as salvage: a stripe, a pair of goggles,
  a drone staked on a pole.
- **The hood tally counts changes, not kills.** A stroke is a Gift and a ring is
  a Lesson, and the rings are painted bigger because a Lesson is the braver
  story. There are no words on anything; a handprint in ash is the rider's own.
- **The water cart is most of what a war rig is.** The armour, the crown spikes
  and the noise are there so nobody takes the town's water on the road. The rack
  of glass tubes on its side is how they test what they carry.

⚠ The column frightens people on purpose, the same as the trophy road, and
nobody in it ever says otherwise.

## The Under

Before the Under was a place for scavengers, fugitives and things that preferred
not to live in the sunlight, the tunnels were just another part of the city.
Then the city above died, and the Wildblood moved downward.

It suited them. Dark, wet, warm in places and freezing in others, full of
abandoned hospitals, electrical rooms, maintenance tunnels, flooded stations,
forgotten laboratories, old residences and enormous stretches of infrastructure
nobody remembers building.

Most of all it was **changing**. Mould through concrete. Animals adapting.
Mutants appearing. Things living in the dark that had never lived above it.

They read that as proof. The Under is not beneath the city — it is beneath
humanity, and they intend to meet whatever is coming.

So there are shrines in maintenance chambers, mutagen pits in flooded rooms, and
communal dens behind collapsed walls. There are walls painted with handprints,
thousands of them, some human and some unmistakably not.

There are places where they leave food.
There are places where they leave teeth.
And there are places where they leave the dead.

The Long Watch has learned to avoid all three.
