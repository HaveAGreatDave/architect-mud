# One world: rooms inside GLASS

> **Status: step 1 built (the layer, the placement, the streaming and a Modelshop prototype, `__interiors`), the street door as a portal built (the lobby seen through it from the street, painted from a baked picture until its mesh is in), step 2's first cut built (a loaded room seen through its own panes from the street), and step 4's first cut built (plain entry rooms, `ONE_WORLD.entry`), phase A of the conversion built (every room on your storey a doorway takes you to) phase B's first cut (the card, the panels that need no Stage, locked doors) phase E (the storeys, but for the lift's ride), the people of phase C, the lamps of phase D and the kitchen, depots and vat hall of phase F; the rest of the conversion that retires the Room Stage on a desktop is design, laid out under [The conversion](#the-conversion).** It replaces the two-renderer split the [Building Stage](building-stage.md) has today. This doc is the one plan for it: [gl-core.md](gl-core.md) and building-stage.md point here.

## The problem

ROOM and GLASS are two renderers. A room is drawn on its own canvas in metres with its own camera, and the city is a picture GLASS paints for its windows (`room-outlook.js`). The front door swaps one canvas for the other and fades between them. However well the picture is lined up, it's a picture, and the swap at the door is a seam you can feel.

## The answer

Draw the rooms in GLASS 2's own scene: one camera, one depth buffer, one sun. A window is glass with the real city behind it, and a doorway is a doorway.

Rooms load only when you're near them, and further off they're drawn more simply:

| Distance from the room's box | What's drawn |
|---|---|
| up to `INTERIOR_TUNE.near` (0.9 tiles) | the whole room: its mesh from `gl/room-mesh.js`, furniture and all |
| up to `INTERIOR_TUNE.mid` (3.5 tiles) | a lit box: floor, walls and ceiling in the room's tones, its lamp at night |
| further | nothing: the building's own GLASS model stands for it |

A room is built the first frame the layer wants it, `INTERIOR_TUNE.budget` (1) a frame, and let go about four seconds after it drops out of range. At most `maxNear` (8) rooms are whole at once.

A whole room's mesh and its materials' pictures are built in a worker (room-mesh-worker.js, the one the Room Stage bakes ahead in), not on the frame: `meshReady` in room-interiors.js asks for it, and until it's back `interiorsFrame` draws the room as its box. Built on the page it was 40 to 60 ms a room warm and about 200 ms cold, so walking up to a building with a few Stage rooms was a run of long frames. Measured in Chrome on 2026-11-13: the Solenne's 21 rooms took about 14 s of cold worker time between them, and then handed the layer their parts in under a millisecond. The meshes are kept by room and lamp (32 of them), since a push makes the room objects again. With no worker (Node, the gates) a room is built on the spot as before.

## How it fits together (step 1, built)

- `room-view.js` `roomToGlass(L, origin, bearing, floorM)`: the 4×4 from a room's own frame to GLASS tiles about its building's tile, and `roomOrigin(frame, bearing)` for where its front wall stands. These are the window view's own sums (`viewOf`, `inBuilding`), and `scripts/client/interiors-smoke.mjs` holds them to each other.
- `room-interiors.js` `interiorRoomsOf(data)`: a building's rooms (spec, furniture rows and `frame`, as `plugins/roomstage` `frameOf` works it out) as entries with a matrix, a bounding box and `parts(lod)`.
- `glass/interiors-near.js`: `pickInteriors` (pure; which rooms, at which level) and `interiorsFrame` (moved to the map window).
- `gl/interiors.js`: the GLASS 2 layer. Uploads by key, draws on the scene's depth buffer after the solids, lit by the frame's day and sun and each room's lamp.
- The wiring: `v.interiorRooms` on a view → `interiorUpload` in `windshield.js` → `opts.interiors` through `gl/install.js` → `drawInteriors` in `gl/world.js` and `gl/context.js`.
- The prototype: `__interiors({ zone })` on the Modelshop's street tool (`tools/modelshop/street.js`), fed by `/api/building-rooms`. It stands you in a room with its own building taken off the map, or outside its door with `outside: true`.

## The street door as a portal (built)

From the street a building's model is solid, its doorway included, so the rooms drawn on the scene's depth buffer are hidden behind it. So the rooms behind a street door are drawn a second time, into a target of their own from the same camera, and the doorway is filled from that picture (`drawPortals` in gl/interiors.js). The fill is a quad on the scene's own depth buffer, so a lamp post in front of the door still stands in front of it, and the door's leaves (glass/street-doors.js, decals, drawn after) stand in front of both. The lobby moves with true parallax as you walk past.

- **Which doors.** Every street door the view on foot draws (`v.streetDoors`, its opening read off the model's mass by glass/doorway-measure.js) whose building has rooms in the frame. windshield.js `streetPortals` makes each one's quad at the back of the doorway's reveal and a clip plane a little inside it, so the room's own street wall, which stands on the street edge in front of the facade, is cut away. A room behind a door is drawn only through it; inside a building (`interiorInside`, ONE_WORLD) it's drawn whole as before.
- **⚠ Pulled along the line of sight.** What a model draws in its doorway is mesh lifted toward the eye (`DETAIL_LIFT`), and a quad set a few millimetres off the wall lost to it. The portal's corners move `PORTAL_PULL` (0.03 tiles) toward the eye instead: the same place on the screen, its depth brought forward.
- **⚠ Gamma in the target.** The room's shader writes display colour with `uGlassLinOut` off, and the quad converts as the scene wants, so a linear frame and a plain one get the same room.
- **The closed box.** A room's lod-1 box now has its front wall too: a room whose street door is in a side wall (the Embassy's) is seen across its front wall from the door, and the clip opens the side you look in from.
- **The glass leaves are see-through** (0.34 by day, 0.24 at night), or the lobby was hidden until the door opened.

### The baked picture: the room before it loads

A room's whole mesh is built in a worker and arrives a moment after you come near; until then it's its box. So each entry room with a dressed street door is photographed ahead of time: `npm run lobby:bake` (scripts/lobby/bake.mjs) opens it in the Room Lab through its street door, where the server stands you walking in, and keeps the frame and the camera that took it (`vp`, the room renderer's view and projection in the room's own frame) in `client/game/assets/lobby/`. lobby-still.js `bakedStill` loads them, and the portal pass paints the room with it, projected from that camera: the box's walls, then the mesh's, take the picture's colours wherever the picture saw them, and their own colours past its edges. Through the door the box already looks like the lobby, and when the mesh comes in the furniture takes its true depth without the picture changing.

It needs Chrome (headless, SwiftShader) and the Modelshop, and is a picture of content, not a gate: re-bake after changing how an entry room looks. A room with no picture is its plain box, in its wall's tone (`flags.room.tone.wall`).

### The door on the street edge

`frame.door` (building-graph.js `streetDoorAt`) is where the street door is in the entry room, and room-view.js `anchorOf` stands that point on the street edge, not the entry room's front wall. They were the same only for a door in the middle of the front wall (the Solenne). The Embassy's lobby has its door in its left wall, so the whole building was stood seven metres out in the street; the Meridian's is in its right wall and the Cherry Pit's in its back, which had its rooms fourteen metres outside the tile. The window view (`inBuilding`) and the matrix (`roomOrigin`) share it, and interiors-smoke checks a door in a side wall and one in a back wall lands on the edge.

## The live view (built, the default on a desktop)

Until a room is drawn in GLASS itself, the Room Stage gets the same look another way: `room-outlook.js` with `live` paints the city on the main thread every frame there's glass in view, from the room's own eye (where you stand, your yaw, pitch and lens), under the room's canvas with its panes as holes. There's no picture, no lag and real parallax, and every Stage feature stays. It costs a GLASS frame a frame, as the street on foot does. Phones, and `__roomFaces = true`, keep the six faces painted in the worker.

## Built since the first plan

- **Windows in the shell, near level (step 2's first cut).** From the street a building's model is solid, so a loaded room was hidden behind it except through the street door. Now each room's own panes are portals too, filled the way the street door is:
  - glass/interiors-near.js `roomPanes(spec, L, M)` turns a room's `windows` (or its old single `window`) into quads on its walls about the tile centre, each with the step into the room. room-interiors.js keeps them on the room as `panes`, and `interiorsFrame` moves them to the window's centre.
  - windshield.js `panePortals` makes a portal of every pane facing the eye (the eye outside it), the nearest `INTERIOR_PANES.max` (12), with a clip plane `PANE_CLIP` (0.012 tiles) inside the wall so the room's own wall at the pane goes. A pane portal draws only its own room (`room`), and a pane whose room isn't built yet is left as the model's glass.
  - ⚠ **One portal a wall, not a pane.** A portal draws its room again, and the Solenne lobby's mesh is about 46k triangles, so one draw per pane was 325k triangles for its front. A wall's panes share a plane, so they share one portal (`quads`) and one draw.
  - ⚠ **One portal at a time, in its own patch.** Each portal cuts its rooms by its own plane, so in a shared target a room drawn for a pane in its side wall kept a front wall that stood over the lobby seen through the door. drawPortals now draws each portal scissored to its quads' box on the screen (`screenBox`) and fills it at once, and takes the target off its texture unit before the next one draws into it.
  - Panes are pulled toward the eye by `INTERIOR_PANES.pull` (0.05 tiles, about 0.9 m), more than a door's 0.03, since a room's wall can stand a little behind the model's face. `__interiorPanes = false` in the console turns them off to compare.
  - **Where the room and the model disagree, it shows.** The Solenne lobby's right-hand glass shows the lobby; on its left the model's mass stands in front of the room's pane, so only a strip of floor shows. That's the room or the model to fit ([rooms fit the model](../reference/building-styles.md)), not the layer.
- **The walk, first cut (step 4).** On foot the server sends each nearby building's rooms once with the street window (`plugins/roomstage` `buildingRoomsNear`, `plugins/freelook` `interiorsFor`), and the street view draws them. When a `room_view` arrives for a room one-world takes, `freelookTakeRoom` (freelook-view.js) keeps you in GLASS instead of opening the Stage: you stand just inside the door, the rooms' floors and furniture hold you (room-layout.js `roomFree`, the Stage's own rule), a doorway between rooms sends its move, and the street door sends yours out.
  - **Which rooms it takes.** `ONE_WORLD = { on: false, entry: true }`, `entry` being the desktop default. glass/interiors-near.js `oneWorldTakes` decides: an entry room off the street with nothing only the Stage does, and since phase A any other room of the building that a doorway on your storey takes you to from inside it in GLASS. `oneWorldBlocker` says why not (lift, rink, open deck, balcony, shop, not an entry room). A phone always opens the Stage (`GL_TIER === 'phone'`). `__oneWorld(true)` takes every drawn room, phone or not; `__oneWorld(false)` none.
  - ⚠ **A storey is the room's matrix height (`M[14]`), never `L.floor`**, which is the room's own floor offset and 0 for nearly every room. Compared by `L.floor`, a whole building was one storey and a step in the lobby could land you in a flat overhead. `sameStorey` is the one comparison.
  - ⚠ **Until 2026-11-21 the street view on foot never had the rooms.** The server sent them, but dispatch.js never passed `msg.interiors` to `openFreelook`; a fresh view didn't keep them either, only a warm one; and the server's `roomsSent` outlived a reconnect, so after a reload it never sent them again. Only the Modelshop prototype ever drew them. Now dispatch passes them to both opens, the view keeps every building sent this session (`sentInteriors`) whichever way it opens, and `player.logout` clears `roomsSent`. interiors-smoke checks all three.
  - ⚠ **A laid-out piece keeps `stock` on itself and drops the row's flags** (room-layout.js `layRows`), so the shop rule read `look.stock` and `serves` and missed every shop until 2026-11-21: Screw It's counter was walked in GLASS with no way to buy. interiors-smoke now checks it.
  - **The building stays on the map, less the storey you're on** (`insideCut`, gl/context.js `uCutA`): the facade, the storeys over you and the doorway are the model's, and this floor's rooms stand in the band the cut takes out. One tile only; the Solenne's other three tiles stay whole.

## The conversion

The end state: on a desktop every room is drawn in GLASS and the Room Stage's canvas, its painted window view and the `ONE_WORLD` switch are gone. Phones keep the Stage until GLASS Lite can carry the rooms. Each phase is a commit or a few, and the game works after every one: a room one-world can't do yet still opens the Stage, so nothing is lost on the way.

### What can go wrong

- **Shader compile.** The ballpark takes about 35 s cold and the figure over a minute. Every feature that adds a program to GLASS's scene adds to the first walk in; prewarm through gl/programs.js.
- **One call site for a big GLSL function.** Inlined five times, one crashed the GPU process on D3D and every ROOM with it. Test on the real GPU, not SwiftShader.
- **Nothing shows through a building.** A room drawn on the scene's depth after the solids is safe; a portal that loses to the model's lift (`DETAIL_LIFT`) shows a hole. Keep the pull and the clip planes.
- **Rooms fit the model.** Where a room and its model disagree, fix the room ([building-styles.md](../reference/building-styles.md)), never the layer.
- **Contexts.** A Stage room with a window and a TV runs four or five WebGL contexts against a cap of 16; one-world should bring that to one. gl-core phase 6 (one GL host) is the same road.
- **Phones.** Nothing in A to H changes the phone path; `PHONE_TIER` keeps the Stage.

#**The room button (2026-11-22).** On a desktop the d-pad's room button is the way into the 3-D world: in a building it sends `room world`, which opens the street view at the building's door and then the room, flagged `world`, for the view to take (dispatch.js waits on `freelookCanTake` up to eight seconds); a room with no street door stands you in its middle. On a street tile it's the street on foot, as before. A phone gets the Stage. Free look stays the staff's camera for flying anywhere.
