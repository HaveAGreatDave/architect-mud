# Deadball's players, second edition

**Status: phases 1 and 2 built (phase 1 2026-11-14, phase 2 2026-11-16), off by default until they've been looked at across a whole game; the rest is design.** The close-up figure hangs on Deadball's rig and draws in the park behind `PARK_FIGURES.on` (the Pose Lab's "figure body" box), in the club's kit, casting its own shadow, with the old body as its fallback (section 1). The decisions table records what David has settled.

The players [deadball-glass2.md](deadball-glass2.md) put in the park are lofted tubes round Deadball's own rig. They play every clip, but up close they read as mannequins: hands are mitts, every batter gets ready the same way, every pitcher throws from one of three slots, and the First Generation's augments and mutations are blobs and rings in their order's colours. This plan rebuilds the players as athletes, with each man recognisable by the way he moves.

## Decided

## What's there now

- **Body:** [ballplayer-mesh.js](../../client/game/js/panels/gl/ballplayer-mesh.js) lofts a torso of rings and tubes down the limbs round the 22 bones of [diamond/rig.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/rig.js), and the GPU skins it with four weights a vertex. The head is the street figure's, with the painted face atlas.
- **Hands:** a hand round the bat is a ridged cylinder. An open hand is a palm with four stiff tubes. There are no finger bones, in Deadball or in the close-up figure, whose sculpted fingers ride the hand bone whole.
- **Motion:** 116 clips under `content/animation_clips/`, keyed in the rig's pose language (wrists, ankles, elbow and knee poles, spine angles) and baked by `npm run clips:bake`. `vary` in [diamond/anim.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/anim.js) gives each man his leg kick and stance width. A pitcher's arm slot is one of three clips (`side` 15%, `three` 45%, `over` 40%), chosen by the hash of his name. Batters share the routine clips (`bat_dig`, `bat_gloves`, `bat_helmet_tap`, `bat_plate_tap`, `bat_point`). A let-go bat flies, lands and skids ([loose-bat.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/loose-bat.js)).
- **First Generation:** [deadball-gen1.js](../../client/shared/deadball-gen1.js) gives each of the 32 a list of gear names, and `gen1Gear` builds each part from blobs, rods, cuffs and rings. Four Arms' second pair has no bones, and Hopscotch's hind legs are a shape on ordinary legs. `gen1Fx` in [gl/ballpark.js](../../client/game/js/panels/gl/ballpark.js) lights them.
- **What already exists to build on:** the close-up figure's muscle relief, sculpted hands, layered clothes and solid hair ([hd-figure.md](hd-figure.md), [sculpted-figure.md](sculpted-figure.md)); its grown bones and `MUTATION_LOOK`, which draw the game's real mutations (`content/mutations/`, 56 of them); the augment catalog (`content/augments/`, 11 of them); and the expressions system ([systems-expressions.md](../systems-expressions.md)).

## 1. The athlete body

The close-up figure gains an `athlete` build and a baseball cut. Deadball stops building its own bodies.

- **Proportions from measurement.** Each position gets a build table from real players' height, weight and segment lengths (a row in the [bibliography](../reference/bibliography.md) when it's sourced): pitchers tall with long levers, catchers thick through the thighs and seat, middle infielders wiry, corner bats heavy in the chest and forearms, a veteran's softer middle. `buildOf` in [cast.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/cast.js) grows from three numbers (girth, shoulders, belly) to a set the figure reads: height, shoulder breadth, chest depth, waist, seat and thigh, forearm, neck, and belly.
- **The skeleton (as built).** The figure hangs on Deadball's own rig instead of the clips moving to the figure's. The park solves a pose as it always has, and [diamond/figure-rig.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/figure-rig.js) turns the solve into the figure's bone rows: each figure bone follows the Deadball bone that does its job, axis for axis, stretched along it to Deadball's length (Deadball's man has legs about 9% longer than the figure's) and scaled across it by his size. Every row takes the figure's joint to Deadball's, so the 116 clips, the IK and anim-smoke are untouched. The head goes exactly where ballplayer-mesh.js puts its own, so the caps, helmets and masks grown off that skull still fit. The gate is `scripts/shapes/figure-rig.mjs`: every clip, sampled through at two heights, every joint and the head within a hundredth of a foot, and no row mirrored.
- **What he wears over it.** ballplayer-mesh.js builds a man in two halves (`look.part`): the body the figure replaces, and the gear it wears (the cap or helmet, the mask, the catcher's gear, the glove, the bat, a First Generation man's parts). In figure mode the park draws the gear on its own palette and the figure from [gl/ballpark-figures.js](../../client/game/js/panels/gl/ballpark-figures.js), in the park's light (the sun, the towers and the sky as the room light's lamps and ambient, `uStreet` 3 handing the float target linear light). The jersey is figure.js's `jersey` cut.
- **Hands and arms (as built, 2026-11-15).** Every row is a turn and one scale, because the figure skins with dual quaternions and takes a bone's scale off its first row: the first rows stretched a limb along its length only, which bent the rotation the shader built and twisted and swelled the arms on screen. A limb is now scaled whole by Deadball's length for it, so the joints still meet and the forearms and thighs come out a little thicker. A hand round a bat is hung by the hole its fingers close round (`GRIP_HOLE`), on the handle's axis, in a power grip: the shape's `gk` moves the fingers' bend from the knuckle to the two finger joints (gl/figure-hands.js `GRIP_FLEX`), so they wrap the handle instead of sweeping through it. The first grip put the handle 3 cm off-centre through the fingers. The figure's thumb bends only at its root, too far from the handle to wrap it, so it's laid up the bat's line, about 30° off it and clear of it; swung across the palm, it stood out sideways like a spike. Each hand holds the bat whichever way round leaves its wrist the smaller turn on its forearm, held between frames so it doesn't flicker (`GRIP_TURN_HOLD`): both hands overhand, as on a broom, the top hand's thumb points toward the knob, and held the other way the top hand stood 160° to 180° round from its forearm in the stance and its wrist's skin was blended the long way round into a spike. Twelve clips still pass a real wrist's 120° for a frame or two as the bat whips through, or longer where one hand holds the bat out alone (bat_point); the rig places the fist by the bat and the wrist alone, so a roll of the forearm in the clips is phase 2's, and the gate ratchets those twelve. A gloved hand is tucked into the glove, and each forearm turns along its length to meet its hand (`forearmTwist`, the shader's `uHandX`), so a turned wrist doesn't wring. The gate holds all four, on the built hand's own vertices bent as the shader bends them: the rows, no finger or thumb in the handle, the wrist within a centimetre of the forearm, and no twist left at the wrist. And a kit's cloth is new each game: the street's wear flecks (which covered the whole undershirt, painted as a sleeve below the jersey's cuff) stay off it.
- **The fallback.** A man is drawn as the figure only once his figure is built in the worker (figure-ahead.js) and its shader has linked; until then he's the old body, so nobody blinks out. The shadows are still the old body's.
- **The kit (as built).** The figure's cloth takes the park's own kit atlas (diamond/kits.js), the same canvas the old body wore, so a club's kit, wordmark, number, surname, pinstripes and piping arrive unchanged and repaint when the club's kit does. gl/figure-hd.js `kitCloth` maps it, in its one call, before the folds and ink go on: the jersey round the body, its rows by Deadball's torso with the figure's joints laid on Deadball's, so the wordmark sits across the chest and the number in the middle of the back; each sleeve round its arm; the trousers round each leg, the knee three quarters down the cell; and the socks on the boot's shaft. Buttons are drawn down the placket. The jersey cut has no front of its own, and the street's wear and denim fading stay off the kit. The figure's belt is on, in the kit's belt colour. The umpires and the grounds crew keep their plain colours.
- **The rest of the kit (as built).** The club's crest patch is painted on the outside of the left sleeve off the park's logo atlas, where the old body's stood. The lettering stands off the cloth as tackle twill, the atlas's brightness bending the normal while a texel is bigger than a pixel, as the old body's `uTwill` does. The jersey has an undershirt to the wrist in the club's colour: figure.js grew an `undersleeve`, built before a cut's own sleeve so the short sleeve hulls it.
- **Hair under the cap (as built).** Every man keeps his own cut (ballplayer-mesh.js faceFor's style), worn under figure.js's `ballcap`, which draws nothing and cuts the figure's locks back under the park cap's band line (`capBand`), so long hair falls from under it and nothing comes out through the crown.
- **Shadows (as built).** A man drawn as the figure casts it into the towers' and the sun's maps (figure-hd.js `depth`, the figure's own vertex shader and no colour), with his gear; until that program has linked he casts his old body.
- **Builds.** The umpires (in the `oxford`, navy over grey) and the coaches take the figure's `dadbod` build from the belly diamond/cast.js gives them, the grounds crew a work shirt. The plate umpire's ball bags are gear, so they hang on the figure.
- **The Player Lab** is the Pose Lab's "who" box: any man by name (the First Generation listed) as the batter, the pitcher and the catcher, in any clip, with "figure body" for the figure.
- **The gate** also builds every cut the park dresses at both levels, the heaviest build and the longest hair, and fails one over its budget (26,000 triangles `hd`, 11,500 `lite`) or a lock above the cap's band.
- **The light, matched.** `PARK_FIGURES`' strengths were set against the old body in the same shots, by the brightness of a white home kit's torso: the towers at 5 by night, the sky's ambient at 2.5 by day, and by day with no low sun the sky's sun direction as the figures' key. Side on and from the mound camera they read within a few levels of the old body.
- **Still to do before it's on by default:** a look across a whole game in the Grandstand (every role, both kits, day and night, the comic look off), and the figure's first frames on a phone.
- **What has to hold.** Every clip plays on the new body with the anim-smoke limits as they are: no foot slides on a plant, no contact error at the glove or the bat, no joint past its limit.
- **Cost.** About 28 people are on the field at once. The figure's `hd` level (about 22,000 triangles) is drawn only when a man stands taller than `PARK_FIGURES.hdPx` on the screen, and its `lite` level (about 9,000) otherwise.

## 2. Hands

- **Finger bones for everyone.** Each finger gets three bones and the thumb three, 15 a hand, skinned in the figure, so ROOM's people gain them too. The street crowd's bake leaves them out.
- **The jointed hand (as built, 2026-11-15).** Every figure's hand is a pose of twenty angles (gl/figure-hands.js `handPose`): for each finger its knuckle, middle joint and last joint and how far it swings forward of its neighbours, and for the thumb its root, its swing across the palm, its knuckle and its last joint. They're bones in all but name: the shader folds each point at every joint it's past, from the tip back, each blended in over a few millimetres (gl/figure-hd.js `handXf`, `uHandL` and `uHandR`), off the tags figure.js already gives every finger and thumb vertex, so no vertex format changed, nothing is added to the skeleton's rows, and ROOM's people have them too. `bendPoint` does the same in JS for the gates. The fingers fold exactly as they did; what's new is the thumb, which bent only at its root as one curve and now has three hinges (figure.js `thumbJointsOf`). A shape in curls still works and is shared out over the joints (`THUMB_SPLIT`); a shape can give its thumb joint by joint (`tj`) or the whole pose (`j`), and shapes with poses mix joint by joint. The first use is the bat: the thumb now wraps the handle beside the index finger with its pad on the wood, where it used to lie up the bat's line a centimetre clear, and figure-rig.mjs holds the pad within a millimetre and a half of the wood. Not yet seen in ROOM: its people's thumbs follow the old curve shared over three joints, close to but not exactly as it was.
- **Hand shapes as a library.** A shape is a set of finger curls and spreads, keyed in clips like any other part of a pose.
  - Bat grips: standard, choked up, knob in the palm, pinky over the knob, and a loose grip for the waggle.
  - Pitch grips: four-seam, two-seam, curve, slider, circle change, splitter and knuckleball. The grip matches the pitch the server chose.
  - Glove hand, relaxed, pointing, fist, open in celebration, clapping, and the catcher's signs. Umpires get the punch-out fist and the flat palms of safe.
- **The library (as built, 2026-11-15).** [diamond/hands.js](../../client/game/js/panels/diamond/hands.js) `HANDS`: the bat grip and a loose one for the waggle, the glove, free, the ball, a fist with the thumb across, a point, flat (the umpire's safe), open (celebrating), cupped for a clap or a high five, a thumb up, the catcher's signs one to four, and two outs (the index and little finger). A clip keys them in a `hands` list, `{ t, L?, R? }` by name, each held till the hand's named again and closed into over `HAND_BLEND` (0.12 s); `''` gives the hand back to what it would be. The schema refuses a name that isn't in the library, the bake keeps them, `sampleClip` puts them on the pose (swapped for a left-hander) and `mixPose` mixes them across a fade, so a hand opens into the next clip's shape instead of snapping. A hand round the bat takes only a bat grip from a clip, a gloved hand and a hand on the ball none. Keyed so far in 17 clips: every umpire's call (the fist, the punch-out point, flat palms for safe, foul and a dead ball), the catcher's signs, a fist pump, a leap with the hands open, a high five, a clap, two outs, an argument, and the stance's loose grip closing for the load. figure-rig.mjs checks every library shape is a sound pose, every name a clip keys is in it, and every bat grip clears the handle. The loose grip was first eased at the knuckle alone, as a fist opens, and its fingertips came round into the wood; it's eased as a power grip is.
- **The catcher's signs carry the pitch (as built, 2026-11-15).** One finger the fastball or sinker, two the curve, three the slider or cutter, four the change, splitter or knuckleball (hands.js `PITCH_SIGNS`). With a man on second he puts down three and the pitcher takes the second; otherwise the one. The director lays them over the catch_sign clip's own as a `hands` layer (world.js), which keys only the hand it names. diamond-smoke.mjs checks the sign he's showing is the pitch's, about 90 times a seed.
- **The ball in the hand, solved (as built, 2026-11-15).** The ball sat where Deadball's old hand held it, 4.6 cm out of the palm on the hand's line, which on the figure was 2 cm off the palm with the ring and little fingers 2 to 3 cm into it. It now sits where the figure's fingers meet it (figure-rig.js `BALL_HOLD`, fitted on the hand's own skin): out on the fingers, the four-seamer's index and middle fingertips and the thumb's pad on it. The director puts it there along his hand bone's axes (hands.js `BALL_IN_FINGERS`, so it needn't load the figure), and every pitch grip's fingers are curled till their tips meet it. So the grips differ in how the fingers spread and which are on the ball, not in how hard they're curled; the curve's index is lifted clear. figure-rig.mjs fails any finger of any grip more than a millimetre into the ball, the four-seamer's three more than two off it, and the director's spot more than a hundredth of a foot from the figure's.
- **Where the hands sit on the bat (as built, 2026-11-16).** Choked up, knob in the palm and pinky over the knob are where the hand sits on the bat, not its shape, so they're a trait of the man rather than a shape in the library: diamond/anim.js `gripOf`, off his name, gives him the standard grip (55%), choked up 0.2 ft (15%), the knob in his palm (18%) or his little finger over the knob (12%), as `BAT_GRIPS`. It's how far the bat slides through his hands (`bat.slide`, rig.js): the hands stay where the clip keyed them and the knob moves, so every grip's wrists are the clip's own, and a choked-up bat is shorter out of his hands. anim-smoke.mjs poses every batting clip at both ends of the range. The knuckleball is the first grip given joint by joint (`j`): the index and middle fingers folded hard at their middle joints, fitted till their tips meet the ball.
- **The wrist past 120°: the swivel, tried and taken out (2026-11-15).** Twelve clips turn a hand on the bat further on its forearm than a forearm turns. A real arm takes that by swinging its elbow round the line from the shoulder to the wrist, so the figure's arm was swung that way, the least that brought the forearm back to 95°. Sampled 40 times a clip instead of the gate's 12, it brought six of the twelve inside a real wrist all through. But it moved the elbow up to 0.85 ft, and when a hand turns over on the bat (`GRIP_TURN_HOLD`), the swing it needed changed at once and the elbow jumped a third of a foot in a frame, which looks worse than a wrist wrung for a frame or two. It came out. Two things it found: the gate's 12 samples a clip miss most of a swing's fastest frames, and the grip turning over is itself a pop of the hand half round the handle in one frame.
- **The grip round the bat, refitted (2026-11-15).** From behind the batter the bat showed across his hands. The hold point had been fitted only so no finger went into the handle, and measured on the hand's own triangles the hand closed round 150° of it: the palm on one side, the fingers on the other, and the handle cutting through the ball of the thumb. Three things changed. The handle is now a real bat's, 0.037 to 0.04 ft round (1.1 to 1.2 cm, ballplayer-mesh.js `BAT_DOMED`), where it was a fifth too thick for the figure's fingers to close round. The grip is given joint by joint (hands.js `bat`), the knuckles and middle joints folded about 52° and the last joints 69°. And the handle lies across the palm on a slant (figure-rig.js `GRIP_HOLE`, `tilt`). It now closes round 340° of the handle within 5 mm, nothing more than a millimetre in, and figure-rig.mjs fails a grip under 330° (the waggle's under 310°). The figure's wrist sits a centimetre short of Deadball's and its forearm is drawn to it (`gripWrist`). That still left a seam of handle 3 to 5 mm wide between the fingertips and the thumb, seen close from the fingertips' side: a finger here is three straight bones of fixed thickness, and none of its joint angles could close it.
- **The wrist, fixed in the clips (2026-11-15).** Given the wrist and the bat, the only freedom an arm has is where its elbow points, and the clips keyed elbows that fought their grip: in the stance the top forearm was turned 162° on its hand, which the park hid by turning the hand over on the bat. [elbow-fit.mjs](../../scripts/shapes/elbow-fit.mjs) re-keys the elbow pole of every key that holds a bat or a rod, in all 26 such clips, the least that brings that forearm's roll inside 60°, with both hands held thumb to the barrel in every clip so none turns over at a hand-off. It keeps every joint limit a key kept, keeps the elbow out of his body, keeps each direction within the schema's turn of its neighbours, and makes a change cost more the closer the keys are; then it runs anim-smoke's own elbow checks on each clip and eases the keys round any failure back toward the authored pose. Measured through the park's own path at 60 frames a clip, the frames past a real forearm's 120° across those clips went from 101 to 20, and the hands turning over on the bat mid-clip from about 33 to 28. The stance, the load, the take, the check, both run-outs, the bat flip, the hit-by-pitch, the on-deck swing and the fishing clips are inside a real wrist all through. What's left is the three swings and the strikeout, where the bat whips round the top hand's forearm faster between keys than any key can hold, and `bat_walk`, whose keys are far apart; the gate now samples 40 frames a clip and ratchets those five. Still to do: more keys through a swing's contact, and the grip's turn over eased across a few frames rather than in one.
- **Fingers that press on what they hold (as built, 2026-11-15).** A hand can be given a cylinder it closes round (gl/figure-hands.js `holdPush`, the shader's `uHoldL` and `uHoldR`), and any finger or thumb skin bent into it is laid on its surface instead, the way flesh flattens against wood. A hand on the bat holds its handle (figure-rig.js `GRIP_HOLD`); every other hand holds nothing and bends free, so ROOM's people are untouched. With it the fingers curl as far as a real fist, each finger fitted joint by joint within a real hand's range (the knuckles to 90°, the middle joints 110°, the last joints 80°): the index and middle fingers hard at the knuckle with their tips round to the thumb, the ring and little fingers more at the last joint. Measured as a camera sees it (the 3 mm cells round and along the handle with no hand skin outside them), two cells a row of 24 show through the grip, at the seam and where the hand ends, where five did with the fingers bending free and nine with the grip before; no finger is pressed more than 3.5 mm flat. figure-rig.mjs holds both: under 2.5 cells a row for the grip (3.5 for the waggle's) and no finger pressed more than 4.5 mm. On screen from behind the batter, the handle now goes into his fists and nothing crosses them. The elbows were fitted again to this grip (elbow-fit.mjs): five frames past a real wrist's 120° across the 26 clips, in the three swings and the strikeout.
- **Contact, solved.** Fingers close round the handle until they touch it, so a choked-up grip and a bare-knuckled one both wrap the bat. The ball is held the same way. The gate measures fingertip gaps.
- **Built so far (2026-11-15):** the power grip round a bat (section 1, "Hands and arms"), and a ball held in the four-seam grip: a free hand within `BALL_IN_HAND` of the ball holds it (figure-rig.js `handHolds`, off the park's `setBall`), so a pitcher in his set and a fielder about to throw have it in their fingers. Until this build the pitcher had no ball at all before the release: the director now gives him one (director.js `pitcherBall`), in his glove as he walks back with it, sliding into the fingers of his throwing hand as his hands come together in the set (where he takes his grip on it), and there to the release, where the pitch's own path takes over. It's null from the release on, so a replay asking where the ball will be at its key moment gets the pitch, not his hand. diamond-smoke.mjs checks it's in his hand as his arm comes through, at about 1,800 moments a seed. The pitch reaches the picture now: the pitcher's ball carries its pitch type (`grip` on the path), the grandstand passes it to the park's `setBall`, and his fingers take that pitch's grip (figure-rig.js `PITCH_GRIPS`, one for each of flight.js's eight types: four-seam, two-seam on the sinker, cutter, slider, circle change, curve, splitter and knuckleball), so the close-up of his hand coming out of the glove is a live tell. A ball in anyone else's hand is held as a four-seamer. figure-rig.mjs fails a pitch type with no grip. The finger bones are still the shader's bend (gl/figure-hands.js), not skinned bones.
- **Close-ups that use them:** the grip turning in the glove during the set, the catcher's signs from behind the plate, and the batting-glove strap. The grip shot is a live tell: the director cuts to it now and then before a pitch, and the booth gets lines to call it ("that's the splitter grip"), sorted to agree with the pitch the way 3a sorts the lines for a ball in play.
- **The grip shot (as built, 2026-11-15).** One pitch in four (`GRIP_ONE_IN`) that the pitcher comes set for on camera from the stretch, with no look over at a runner, the centre-field camera goes tight on his hands in the glove, 2.2 ft top to bottom on a 0.3° lens (camera.js `grip`, director.js `gripWindow`). His grip is hidden in the glove there, as a pitcher means it to be. It's his arm coming back that shows it, the ball in his fingers turned to centre field, so as his hands part the lens widens, leaning after the ball, to the centre-field shot's own framing just after the release, and the director cuts to `cf` there without the picture moving. That pitch is seen from behind him whichever pitch camera the viewer picked. Never from a windup: he faces the plate, and centre field has his back. To make it possible the pitcher keeps the ball in his fingers through the set instead of losing it in the glove, and the ball is drawn at its true size once it's 24 pixels across or more (ballpark.js `ballSize`), so a broadcast ball still reads at 1.4 times its size but a close-up one sits in his fingers. diamond-smoke.mjs checks every grip shot: the ball in frame as it cuts in, his hands together till it widens, the ball in the picture as his arm comes back, the widened picture equal to `cf`'s by the cut, and `cf` after; about ten a seed. The booth's lines for it aren't written: the booth's text comes from the server, which doesn't know which pitches the picture shows tight.

## 3. The bat and the swing

- **Each man has his own bat (as built, 2026-11-15).** ballplayer-mesh.js `batOf`, off his name: the wood (pale ash, honey maple, dark-stained birch or painted black, tinting the park's wood texture), the barrel's size within 7%, black, white or brown tape wrapped round the handle as its own sleeve or none (sixty triangles, since the batter's full level was within sixty of its budget), and pine tar worked up from the grip. About 32 bats over 400 men. A bat he lets go of (a flip, a run out) is his too: the world keeps who let it go (`W.batsAt` gives `id`), and the park draws the loose bat in his wood, barrel, tar and tape (ballpark.js `setBats`). Two in five bats are cupped, the end hollowed a quarter-inch on the same rings as a domed end (`batProfile`), so it costs nothing; the knob was always turned.
- **Stance as data:** open or closed, deep crouch or tall, hands high or low, bat flat or straight up, waggle style, and weight back or even.
- **Swing as data:** the load, the stride (toe tap, leg kick or none), the attack angle, a one-handed or two-handed finish, and the helicopter finish. The swing clips blend by these numbers, and a procedural layer moves the hands and bat path onto them, so no two men swing alike.
- **Flips.** The styles are none (he drops it), a set-down, a toss, a spin flip, a launch end over end, and a staredown before he lets go. Each man's temperament (showman, stoic, hothead, old pro) decides when he flips: always on a home run, on a big hit or a walk if he's a showman, never if he's a stoic. Flight comes from the hands' last frames, as loose-bat.js does now, with the spin the style gives.

## 4. Pitchers

- **A delivery is a set of numbers,** settled by the pitcher's name, or by his row for a First Gen man: arm angle (submarine to over the top), release height, extension, which side of the rubber, stride length and line (crossfire or closed), tempo, leg kick (slide step, high kick, double kick, turn-back), glove action, head movement, and the finish (falls off to the side, or finishes square).
- **On screen.** The three slot clips blend by arm angle, and a fourth clip adds the submarine pitcher. At the release event the hand is moved to the computed release point, and the pitch's flight starts there.
- **In the sim.** The numbers move to a shared module that the server's `baseball.js` imports alongside `traitOf`. Extension adds perceived speed, an odd or low slot adds deception, and sidearm sharpens the platoon split. Each effect is small, and the [simulation grading](../reports/simulation-grading.html) is run before and after to check league averages hold.
- **Between pitches:** the rosin bag, a cap tug, a glove to the face, a walk round the mound, and how long he stares in for the sign. These come from the same parts library the batters use.

## 5. Personality

One record per man, settled by his name, pure, imported by server and client:

- **Routine:** which parts he does, in what order, how many practice swings, and how often he steps out. The routine changes with the count, so he steps out after a strike he didn't like.
- **Kit:** socks high or trousers to the shoe, sleeve length, cap set straight or tilted, undershirt colour, a shirt tail out on a veteran.
- **Gear:** batting gloves or bare hands, wristbands, elbow and shin guards, eye black, sunglasses, a chain.
- **Chewing:** gum (with the odd bubble on the bench), sunflower seeds, tobacco, or nothing. The jaw keeps moving through his idles.
- **Face:** expression beats at the moments that matter: effort at the release, a grimace on a swing and miss, a roar on a home run, a glare at the umpire, a smirk on a flip. These play through the expressions system's faces.
- **Hair and beard:** hair showing under the cap and longer hair out the back, the helmet coming off with his hair under it, beards and stubble from the sculpted head.

Routine parts are short clips that start and end in a pose his stance can join. The composer strings them together and the clip player crossfades them, as it does now. The First Gen men and a handful of named stars get signature routines keyed by hand over the composed one.

## 6. Uniforms

- **Shells over the body:** the undershirt, the jersey (button placket, piping, sleeve cut), the trousers (bloused at the knee or long), belt and loops, stirrups and sanitary socks, and cleats.
- **Lettering with thickness:** numbers, names and the wordmark as tackle twill, raised, with a stitched edge in the paint. The club's kit and crest come through [club-crest.js](../../client/game/js/panels/club-crest.js) as they do now.
- **Cloth:** a fabric normal map (knit), folds driven by the joints (inside the elbow, behind the knee, at the waist on a twist), and the jersey's tail swaying.
- **Wear over a game:** dirt already collects. It gains grass stains and slide marks placed where his body touched the ground, sweat darkening the collar and back late in the game, and a dusty face after a head-first slide.

## Phases

1. **The athlete body.** The figure's `athlete` build and baseball cut, the retargeted clips, and the Player Lab. Nothing looks worse than now, and the anim gates pass unchanged.
2. **Hands and the bat (built 2026-11-16).** Finger bones, the hand-shape library, solved contact, each man's own bat and grips. Carried forward: the swings' last few frames past a real wrist and the grip turning over in one frame (to phase 3's release work, which re-keys the arms), the booth's lines for the grip shot (the server has to know which pitches the picture shows tight), and a look at ROOM's people's new thumbs.
3. **Pitchers.** The delivery as data, the blended slots and the submarine clip, release by IK, and the sim change graded.
4. **Personality.** The persona record, the routine composer, stances and swings, flips, kit and gear, chewing, and faces.
5. **The First Generation.** The four orders' builds, materials, effects and own clips, and the renames.
6. **Finish.** Cloth folds, stains and sweat, and the close-up and replay cameras that show the work off.

Each phase runs behind a switch until it beats the one before it on the same shots.

Umpires, coaches and the grounds crew move to the new body in phase 1 with the players: the umpires with their chest protectors, masks and call styles, the coaches in windbreakers flashing signs with real fingers, the crew in work clothes. The athlete build keeps hockey in mind (a skater's crouch, pads under the sweater) so the CPhL can take it up later, but that's its own project.
