# 60 fps on a busy street

**Status: steps 0, 1 and 5 built (three fixes, a bench that measures Lite, and the marks and kit parts kept on the GPU), step 3 started, step 6 measured and not needed; steps 2, 4 and 7 are design. The reflection reuse and Settings → Graphics are built.**

A cab on Marrow Street runs at about 15 fps in play. 60 fps is 16.7 ms a frame. This is the plan to get there on a desktop, step by step, each step landing with a route-bench number. It's the CPU half of [gl-core.md](gl-core.md) phase 2, narrowed to one target.

## Where the frame goes

`__benchRoute('marrow-cab')` in the Modelshop, RTX 2070 SUPER, 640×360, a warm page, measured 2026-11-24 after step 0. Automatic picks GLASS Lite on every device (`setGlassMode` in `windshield.js`), so Lite is what players get; the baseline in `scripts/perf/baselines/` is Full.

| | Lite | Full |
|---|---|---|
| CPU p50 / p95 / p99 (ms) | 14 / 25 / 35 | 15 / 30 / 59-89 |
| GPU p50 (ms) | 3.6-4.1 | 4.7-4.9 |
| `world:build` | 6.4 | 7.9-8.8 |
| of which `world:arms` | 1.9-2.1 | 3.8-4.5 |
| of which `world:street` | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| `world:gl` (the whole GL hook, CPU) | 6.5-7.0 | 7.1-7.3 |

**On this route the renderer already makes 60 fps at the median on Lite.** What it misses is the p95 and p99, which are the map window recentring. So a 15 fps Marrow in play is mostly something the bench leaves out: the people (the `actors` pass reads 0 here), a full-size canvas, and the rest of the client around `paintWindshield`.

⚠ **A fresh page's Lite run was Full without the bake.** Lite's chunks load by fetch and the bench paints a route in one synchronous loop, so until 2026-11-24 the first Lite run on a page drew every building from its arm (20 ms, arms 6.3). `__benchRoute` now waits on `liteReady` (gl/lite.js) and reports `lite: 'ready'`; a run that says `timed out` isn't a Lite number.

**Inside `world:gl`**: GL calls themselves are about 3 ms a frame; draw calls are cheap (169,000 `drawArrays` over a run cost 82 ms). The rest is JavaScript packing records into buffers, most of it `uploadSolids`.

## Step 0: built

- **Fading signal masts kept on the GPU** (`signalMeshEmit`, `RENDER_TUNE.signalFadeKeep`). A mast in the 5-tile fade before FAR was copied into the solids stream every frame: about ten masts, 7,700 faces a frame on Marrow. Now one retained copy per 16 alpha steps.
- **A murmuration's course asked once per knot** (`flockFrame` in `murmur.js`): 29 calls to the caller's `course` a step instead of 52.
- **`perchAim` kept for the window** (`RENDER_TUNE.perchAimKeep`): `landSite` was most of a murmuration's CPU, asked for the same two leg ends on every sample.

## No CPU copy of what's on the GPU (built 2026-11-25)

The retained arenas (gl/solids.js `retain`, and gl/retain.js for sprites, strokes and decals) kept a `Float32Array` mirror of everything they'd sent, at twice the working set, though nothing ever read it back: a rebuild writes every kept group again from its own records. Each now stages only what it's writing: an append's new groups in a small kept buffer (up to 4 MB; past that one of its own), a rebuild's in a buffer of exactly its size that's dropped after, uploaded with the stream's new `writeAt` into space `reserve` set aside. Headless on Marrow, the solids arena's CPU copy went 24.5 MB to 0.3 on foot and 14.3 to 0.5 in a cockpit, and all typed arrays 183 MB to 156 on foot; the GPU's buffers are the same size. `retain-check` (which mirrors every buffer write and compares triangles), `retain-world-check` and `lite-retain` pass. What this buys is heap and GC pressure, the p99, not the median; the next step of the same kind is the kit's and the kept arms' JavaScript records themselves (80,000 faces of objects for the kit alone), which a rebuild still needs unless the arena compacts on the GPU (`copyBufferSubData`).

## Rigid moving parts kept on the GPU, moved by a matrix (built 2026-11-25)

A part that only turns or slides was redrawn and streamed every frame. Now a retained solids group can carry a placement, `bayGroup(recs, ka, xf)`: 16 numbers, column-major, in the window's frame, which the solids shader applies as `uGroupM` to the points and normals (gl/solids.js; the identity for everything else, so nothing else moves). The part is drawn once at rest and kept; only the matrix changes.

- **The footbridge lift cars** (`RENDER_TUNE.kitCarsGPU`): the kit's dynamic pass has a `cars` mode that draws only the cars, at rest, and `carsApart` leaves them out of `parts`, whose key then no longer holds the car's travel. The cars are kept per lamp state and rise by `kitCarRise` (street-kit.js); both cars move together, so one matrix.
- **An AA battery's guns and radar** (`RENDER_TUNE.aaGPU`): aa-emplacement.js tags each moving face with its frame (`yaw`, `lift|base|trunnion`, `pintle`, `radar`), and each frame writes its matrix from rest (`aaPose`). The frame's own pass (`noFaces`) still slews the guns, lights the lamps and fires; a `rest` pass draws the moving faces at bearing, elevation and spin 0, kept per battery (`AA_REST`), and drawAAMark moves each matrix from the camera's frame into the window's.
- On foot on Marrow (`movecost.mjs marrow foot --gl --lite --tally`): loose records 1,866 to 1,587 a frame, floats written 359k to 327k.
- Exact: `exact.mjs aaGPU 0 1 --unordered` over 60 views, and `exact.mjs kitCarsGPU 0 1 --unordered --lifts`, where `--lifts` starts every footbridge lift 2.5 s before the frozen clock so the cars are compared mid-ride. scene.mjs flattens a placed group through its matrix as the shader does. A matrix is a plain array, not a Float32Array: rounded on the CPU it read 6e-9 off.
- ⚠ **Anything on the CPU that reads BAY_SINK sees a placed group at rest.** The prop shadows did (propshadow.js `bayCasterSplit` took every group as a fixed caster), and now cast a placed group from its matrix (`placedCasters`, cached per matrix). armkeep-check's fake GL reads no uniform, so it runs with `kitCarsGPU` and `aaGPU` off, as it does `kitFadeGPU`.
- Left: the chevrons (a scroll in the shader), the radar arm and the lighthouse beam (wires and a light, not solids), and the crane jibs and the masher's ram, which are live parts drawn with `movingBox` and wires.

## Settings → Graphics (built 2026-11-25)

The tablet's Settings has a Graphics tab: the frame rate for every view (`client/shared/frame-pace.js`, which stands in for `requestAnimationFrame` when the choice isn't Vsync: caps at 60 or 30 on any refresh rate, or Uncapped, a 0 ms timer loop that shows the real paint rate on the perf meter; a browser can't switch vsync off, so the screen still shows frames on its refresh), and GLASS's options through `setGfx` in windshield.js: anti-aliasing (MSAA, FXAA, both, TAA), resolution (`dprMax` as a share of the screen's ratio), reflections, sun shadows, ambient occlusion, bloom, clouds and the scene governor. Each is Automatic by default, meaning the tune this device gets without asking, PHONE_TIER included, taken the first time `setGfx` runs. Renderer, Cel Shading, Shadows, Depth of Field and the Perf Meter moved onto the tab. For a measurement, Uncapped and Adaptive Detail off take the governors out of the number.

## How each step is measured

- Run `__benchRoute('marrow-cab')` with `glassLite` 1 and 0, before and after, in the same freshly loaded page. A page that has run a dozen benches drifts up to twice as slow.
- Report `cpu`, `allPhases` and, for arm work, `items`.
- Every step lands behind a `RENDER_TUNE` switch, so the old path stays one setting away for an A/B.
- Add a crowded route once step 4 starts, with a new name: changing an existing route breaks every baseline that names it (`tools/modelshop/routes.js`).
- **`marrow-foot`** is the street on foot: the cab's Marrow route at the street view's eye, `FOOT_TUNE` and near plane (`client/game/js/panels/foot-tune.js`, shared with freelook-view.js), and `people` pedestrians seeded on open ground beside it (`routePeople`).
