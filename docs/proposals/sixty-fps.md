# 60 fps on a busy street

**Status: steps 0 and 1 built (three fixes, and a bench that measures Lite), step 3 started; steps 2 and 4 to 7 are design.**

A cab on Marrow Street runs at about 15 fps in play. 60 fps is 16.7 ms a frame. This is the plan to get there on a desktop, step by step, each step landing with a route-bench number. It's the CPU half of [gl-core.md](gl-core.md) phase 2, narrowed to one target.

## Where the frame goes

`__benchRoute('marrow-cab')` in the Modelshop, RTX 2070 SUPER, 640×360, a warm page, measured 2026-11-24 after step 0. Automatic picks GLASS Lite on every device (`setGlassMode` in `windshield.js`), so Lite is what players get; the baseline in `scripts/perf/baselines/` is Full.

| | Lite | Full |
|---|---|---|
| CPU p50 / p95 / p99 (ms) | 14 / 25 / 35 | 15 / 30 / 59-89 |
| GPU p50 (ms) | 3.6-4.1 | 4.7-4.9 |
| `world:build` | 6.4 | 7.9-8.8 |
| of which `world:arms` | 1.9-2.1 | 3.8-4.5 |
| of which `world:street` | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| `world:gl` (the whole GL hook, CPU) | 6.5-7.0 | 7.1-7.3 |

**On this route the renderer already makes 60 fps at the median on Lite.** What it misses is the p95 and p99, which are the map window recentring. So a 15 fps Marrow in play is mostly something the bench leaves out: the people (the `actors` pass reads 0 here), a full-size canvas, and the rest of the client around `paintWindshield`.

⚠ **A fresh page's Lite run was Full without the bake.** Lite's chunks load by fetch and the bench paints a route in one synchronous loop, so until 2026-11-24 the first Lite run on a page drew every building from its arm (20 ms, arms 6.3). `__benchRoute` now waits on `liteReady` (gl/lite.js) and reports `lite: 'ready'`; a run that says `timed out` isn't a Lite number.

**Inside `world:gl`**: GL calls themselves are about 3 ms a frame; draw calls are cheap (169,000 `drawArrays` over a run cost 82 ms). The rest is JavaScript packing records into buffers, most of it `uploadSolids`.

## Step 0: built

- **Fading signal masts kept on the GPU** (`signalMeshEmit`, `RENDER_TUNE.signalFadeKeep`). A mast in the 5-tile fade before FAR was copied into the solids stream every frame: about ten masts, 7,700 faces a frame on Marrow. Now one retained copy per 16 alpha steps.
- **A murmuration's course asked once per knot** (`flockFrame` in `murmur.js`): 29 calls to the caller's `course` a step instead of 52.
- **`perchAim` kept for the window** (`RENDER_TUNE.perchAimKeep`): `landSite` was most of a murmuration's CPU, asked for the same two leg ends on every sample.

## The plan

1. **Built: the bench measures Lite.** Lite doesn't run arms for a baked building; the bench did, on a fresh page (above). The marks and live parts that do still run on Lite are about 2 ms of arms.
2. **Measure the game, not the bench.** Started 2026-11-24 in the Browser pane (RTX 2070 SUPER, D3D11, a 572×905 phone-layout pane): a ROOM room in Golden Goose Gardens, warm, runs at 60 fps median with a few 200 ms hitches (p99 200). Opening it cold froze the page for 46 s in one task: the rink's programs include the close-up figure's (two of 205k characters), and `createArenaRenderer` asked `LINK_STATUS` straight away, waiting out D3D's compiler. `warmArena` (gl/arena.js) now compiles them in a throwaway context first and the rink view draws in 2-D till they're done, as `prepareBallpark` does for the park. The rest, attributed with the long-animation-frame API: the street on foot pre-built hidden behind the room (freelook-view.js `warm`, because the Concourse next door has the street door) took 8.5 s in its first paint and 3.8 s more when the Lite bake arrived and the city was built again. Its mass rebuild was 5.5 s: 2.6 s of meshes captured from arms with the bake not yet loaded, 1.4 s baking surfaces, 0.9 s packing the atlas. The warm loop now waits for `warmScenes` (gl/programs.js: GLASS's and ROOM's programs compiled in a throwaway context; the GLASS interiors layer now declares its program) and for `liteReady` round the tile: one 6.8-7.1 s frame and no second build. Then the surface bake was spread: `glBakeBudget` (gl/world.js) lets a caller give the mass build a few ms a frame for its surfaces, kept in the scene's memo, with the build itself left for the frame they're all baked; the warm street sets 8 ms. Its worst frame fell to 0.5 s in one run and 4.7 s in another. What's left is the atlas pack (about 0.9 s), the rest of the mass build and the arms, still in one frame. One run also had an 18.8 s frame in the Stage's own open (usually 2.2 s), which didn't repeat and isn't explained. Also still there: 2.2 s in a promise after `loadCss` as the Stage opens, and 1.6-3.4 s in the Stage's people bake (`room-stage.js`, a setTimeout loop over `bakePeople`). The street on foot, measured 2026-11-24 on Marrow Street at 916,909 in the game (a guest on `test.localhost:3000`, pane 648×767 at DPR 1, Lite on, about 96 street people in the window), runs at 10 to 16 fps, frames 66.7 ms apart at the median; the Zamboni Bay room in the Stage holds a flat 60. With the profiler on, `paintWindshield` is 25 to 45 ms of it: `world:gl` 18 to 28 (the bench's is 6.5), `world:build` 8 to 16, `world:arms` 7. The new `gl:hook` and `gl:blit` marks split `world:gl`: all of it is the GL pass's own JavaScript, and the blit into the 2-D canvas costs nothing (`glTransfer` 0 drops the page to 4 fps, so the transfer is already the right path). Every GPU pass the cab bench leaves out costs CPU in that hook as well: turning off, one at a time, the mirror saved about 8 ms, and SSAO, the near cascade, the cloud shadow and the interiors about 4 or 5 each (single runs, noisy). The GPU sits at 600 to 900 MHz of 2130 and is 22 to 43% busy, so the frame isn't GPU-bound. The street view's own work round the paint is nothing: `__footPerf(true)` (freelook-view.js) splits its callback, and the camera and every one-world tick together are 0.2 ms a frame, so the callback is `paintWindshield`. The gap between a 25-45 ms paint and a 66.7 ms frame is idle main thread, not code. The bench can't see any of this: its routes carry no people, no interiors and the cab's tune, not `FOOT_TUNE`, so the next bench work is a route with all three. The rooms drawn in the street already load on demand: `pickInteriors` (glass/interiors-near.js) keeps a room whole within 0.9 tiles and as a lit box within 3.5, at most 24 and 8 whole, and builds one a frame. On Marrow Street the street view holds 55 candidates and draws one, Marrow House's lobby (60k triangles) through its two portals (`globalThis.__interiorStats = {}` shows it). Inside a building every room of it is kept whole wherever it is, so the layer now skips a room whose box is wholly out of view (`INTERIOR_CULL`, gl/interiors.js `boxOut`; `__interiorCull = false` draws them all). In the Golden Goose concourse it skips 1 room of 6, 158k triangles to 141k; the frame time moved within its noise. ⚠ When the desktop app's window is covered the pane throttles to about one frame a second while `visibilityState` still says `visible`; per-paint numbers are still good then, a rate isn't. Profile a real Marrow drive in the game client at the player's canvas size, with the street's people and everything around `paintWindshield`, and split the frame the same way. The steps below are ordered by what that shows.
3. **Spread the recentre.** Started: most of a recentre's GL cost wasn't building new tiles (no mesh is captured new on Marrow after the first frames) but collecting surface keys face by face over the whole window, about 56,000 faces, 3 to 54 ms. `texKeysOf` in `gl/world.js` keeps each shared face list's distinct keys. `movecost.mjs marrow cab --resend 60 --recentre --intern --gl --lite --prof --frames 400`: `world:gl` on a recentre went from 18-25 ms to 5-11, paint p99 from 55 ms to 28-37. Then the starling field (`murmurObstacles`) brings 32 of its 128 rows up to a new window a frame (`RENDER_TUNE.murmurSpread`), so its 2-11 ms is spread over four frames at about 2 ms each. Paint p90 is now 16 ms and p99 22-29. Then two smaller ones: a traffic signal's mesh is keyed on its world tile and moved on a recentre (`RENDER_TUNE.signalWorld`, held exact by `recentre-exact.mjs signalWorld`), which was every mast rebuilt, 1.7-3.3 ms; and a provisional floor shifts the last seabed depth map instead of building one (`RENDER_TUNE.seabedShift`), 3.5 ms to 1.4 on the recentre frame. Each is 1-2 ms, inside the bench's run-to-run noise on the percentiles (p90 16-18.5, p99 22-29 over four runs). What's left on a recentre is the arms, `uploadSolids` and the street kit, about 1 ms each; the first frame of a run still spikes to about 50. Re-measured 2026-11-24 (`movecost.mjs marrow cab --resend 60 --recentre --intern --gl --lite --prof --rows --frames 400`): paint median 10.6-12.0 ms, p90 16-22, p99 26-41 over three runs. A recentre frame costs 10 to 20 ms more than its neighbours, spread over `world:gl` (+4 to +13), `world:arms` (+2 to +11, sometimes the frame after), the street kit (+3), the ground mesh (+3) and the surface bakes (+5, a frame either side); no one builder is most of it. The GL view's methods by total (`window.__glPT`, through a wrapper round movecost.mjs): `uploadKept`, the mass's new tiles, isn't among the top fourteen, and the same drive without recentring read worse (p99 58, max 146) than with it (p99 29, max 43). So "the new row builds in one frame" no longer holds as the cause: the fixes above took most of it, and what's left is several systems at a few ms each, inside the run-to-run spread. The largest of those, `world:arms`, was tried and taken out the same day: a cap on kept-arm probes a frame (a probe runs the arm about four times; past three a building ran live once and was probed later). Two pairs, cap 3 against none: p99 35.5 and 33.7 ms against 29.0 and 29.4, `world:arms` 2.42-2.47 against 2.26-2.29. Holding probes back keeps buildings live for longer, which costs more over a drive than the bursts it spreads. The original idea, kept for if a later measurement finds a single builder again: when the camera crosses a tile, the new row of tiles builds in one frame. Build it a few tiles ahead over several frames (the street kit's `KIT_AHEAD` does this for the kit), so no frame pays for a whole row. This is the bench's p95 and p99.
4. **Already done, found 2026-11-24.** gl/actors.js draws the pavement people as instanced meshes: one skinned body with its clips baked into textures, one instance record a person, a few draws by head and hair style (the street-people ladder built it). `world:street`, the people included, is 0.5 to 3 ms, and the people's meshes off saved about 2 ms in the game, inside the noise. As first written: **Instance the crowd** before streets get busy: one mesh per body type, one instance record per person, so a crowd costs per person what a parked car costs.
5. **Started 2026-11-24: a kept mark's metals go to the GPU once (`RENDER_TUNE.markMetalKeep`, on).** What the street on foot still streamed every frame was the marks' metal and film faces and the street kit's film (`__solidsTally`, switched off one at a time on `marrow-foot`: marks on the GPU off took the `a, amb, n, p, rgb, tex` records from 1,520 a frame to 217 and the smoothed metal from 920 to 330; the kit off took 1,380, all 689 of the film among them). A mark's metals streamed because the solids layer smooths metal normals on the stream only (gl/solids.js `smooth`), and a retained group is written with whatever `vn` and `m` its records carry. Now armReplay smooths a kept mark's static metals once, among themselves (`smoothMetalSet`, the same code moved to module level as `smoothNormals`), and keeps them as their own retained group; the sky they reflect comes from `markEnvFrame` (MARK_METAL_ENV, built as the lock builds its own) on the list as `metalEnv`, since no streamed metal may be left to bring one. Film still streams: the layer draws it as its own range after the opaque world. `marrow-foot`, Lite, five interleaved runs: streamed smoothed metal 1,097 to 90 faces a frame, all loose records 4,612-4,625 to 3,614-3,638, `uploadSolids` 11.6-13.9 to 7.3-8.7 ms a frame, CPU p50 34.0-39.4 to 25.3-28.5 ms, p95 48.4-55.8 to 39.2-45.2 (a page that had run several benches, so all slower than fresh; compare across only). ⚠ Smoothed apart from what streams beside them, so a corner where a kept metal meets a streamed one isn't shared. The pixel comparison was inconclusive: two renders with the same setting differed by 3,500 to 25,000 pixels (people and the clock), and off against on by 4,551, so a close look at a mark's copper and steel is still to do. ⚠ The "689 film faces" in the kit was a miscount: `__solidsTally`'s `sig` lists a record's keys, and the kit's pooled records carry `film: false`, so they were counted with the glass. Counted by value (`dist` now buckets `film` apart), real glass on `marrow-foot` is about 10 faces a frame either way. A film pass for retained groups was built the same day, measured no change, and taken out again. What the 689 really were: the kit's near-tier moving parts and its fading copies, pooled fresh every frame by design. As first written: **Bake the static marks.** The lot, berth, gate and statue are fixed geometry that runs every frame. gl-core phase 2 step 2 already lists them.
6. **Weighed 2026-11-24 and not done for now.** Under Lite, which players run, most buildings run no arm at all, so `world:arms` (2.2 ms a frame median on the cab drive, 3.3-4.3 on foot) is mostly the marks and the few unbaked or animated tiles; replay is part of that, so the win is perhaps 1 to 2 ms. Letting the GPU cull needs more than back-face culling, which GLASS can't use (its buildings aren't closed, and culling drops the soffits a cab looks up at): a per-face facing test in the shader for the one-sided pieces, so a sign on a turned-away front still vanishes, across the sprite, stroke, decal and solid sinks and the kept-arm checks. Worth it if `armReplay` alone reads over about 3 ms on Lite. As designed: **Stop replaying kept records** (Full, and Lite's unbaked tiles). A kept arm (`armReplay`) is checked face by face against the eye (`faceAway`) and pushed into the sinks record by record every frame, and any facing change throws it away (`faceDrop`). Draw every face and let the GPU cull, so a kept arm goes to the GPU once.
7. **Planned in [glass-worker.md](glass-worker.md):** the whole street-on-foot paint in a worker, as the room's outlook already is, since the world build can't be lifted out of windshield.js's drawing on its own. ⚠ A worker frees the page; it doesn't make the paint cheaper. As first written: **Move the build to a worker**, if 2 to 6 don't reach 16 ms in play. The world build is maths on the map window; a worker sends typed arrays and the main thread only issues GL calls. It's the largest change and the last.

## The effect passes on foot are not a lever (measured 2026-11-24)

In the game, inside the Golden Goose over wet ice with the GPU at low clocks, turning off the mirror, SSAO, the near cascade, cloud shadows or the interiors each read 4 to 8 ms, single noisy runs. On the bench they don't hold. `marrow-foot`, Lite, GPU ms a pass at p50: solids 3.13, mass 1.79, hdr 1.11, actors 1.02, billboards 0.94, SSAO 0.57, prop shadows 0.31, near cascade 0.01, mirror 0 (dry); GPU p50 10.8 against CPU p50 27.4, so the frame is the CPU's. On `glasshouse-cab-rain` the mirror is 1.23 ms of GPU and about 1.8 ms of CPU (p50 12.4 with it, 10.6 without). Trimming effects on foot would cost the look for a millisecond or two; none was changed. The CPU is where the frame goes: the world build and the solids streamed every frame.

## How each step is measured

- Run `__benchRoute('marrow-cab')` with `glassLite` 1 and 0, before and after, in the same freshly loaded page. A page that has run a dozen benches drifts up to twice as slow.
- Report `cpu`, `allPhases` and, for arm work, `items`.
- Every step lands behind a `RENDER_TUNE` switch, so the old path stays one setting away for an A/B.
- Add a crowded route once step 4 starts, with a new name: changing an existing route breaks every baseline that names it (`tools/modelshop/routes.js`).
- **`marrow-foot`** is the street on foot: the cab's Marrow route at the street view's eye, `FOOT_TUNE` and near plane (`client/game/js/panels/foot-tune.js`, shared with freelook-view.js), and `people` pedestrians seeded on open ground beside it (`routePeople`). It leaves out the rooms drawn in the street (gl/interiors.js). First numbers, 2026-11-24, RTX 2070 SUPER, 640×360, Lite ready, `passes: false`: CPU p50 22-25 ms, p95 32-53, GPU p50 7-9, `world:gl` 11-13, `world:build` 10-13. The cab on the same page reads p50 15. 110 people against none moves nothing outside the run-to-run noise, so on the bench the crowd isn't the cost; the gap from the cab is the foot view itself. **What the foot frame costs is the window's size, not an effect.** Turning off, one at a time, the mirror, SSAO, every shadow, the people's meshes, TAA and ink moves `world:gl` by under 1 ms each. The window radius moves everything: `__benchRoute('marrow-foot', { r })` reads CPU p50 22 at r 36 (what the freelook plugin sends on foot, `FLIGHT_RADIUS`), 20 at r 20 and 14 at r 14, though `FOOT_TUNE.farMax` already caps the drawn city at 14. Split by GL view method (`window.__glPT`), r 36 against r 14: `uploadSolids` 8.5 ms against 3.8, `drawActors` 2.9 against 0.7, the rest within 0.5; and in the windshield's own phases `sweep:tiles` +1.5, `world:arms` +1.1, `world:street` +0.6. ⚠ But a radius-14 window isn't a saving to be had: it cuts the picture. `FAR` is distance ahead (`f`), not from the eye, and the view on foot is wide, so a front 14 tiles ahead and 10 aside is 17 off and in view; the window's edge removes it. `globalThis.__solidsTally = { sig: {}, dist: {} }` (gl/context.js `uploadSolids`) counts the loose records streamed each frame: at r 36, 4,605 a frame against 3,160 at r 14, the difference mostly metal faces 12 to 20 tiles out (about 935 a frame in that band) and others out to 36. Those are in the view cone. What makes the street on foot dear is that a walker in a street sees the first row of fronts and almost nothing behind, while Lite has no occlusion (the occluder pass reads 0.003 ms), so everything in the cone is packed and sent. The lever is occlusion on foot, gl-core phase 2 step 6, planned in [foot-occlusion.md](foot-occlusion.md). The CPU occluder doesn't do it: with Lite off it culls 1.7 buildings a frame on `marrow-foot`, and turning it off moved CPU p50 from 33 to 35 ms; emptying the cells past `farMax` (tried and taken out) changed nothing for the same reason. The window can't be cut round its centre on foot in any case: the eye wanders up to `RECENTER_R` (18 tiles) from it before the street view recentres. ⚠ A cold page with the per-pass timers on froze for over 8 minutes on this route; run it with `passes: false` until that's understood.
