# Pets

**Status: phase 1 built (the model, in the Modelshop); phases 2 to 4 started (the `pets` table, Scrappy as an NPC in Cyd's flat, skinned on the GPU in ROOM and living his day there, said in the text world as it happens, up on the furniture, greeting people at the door, the petting ladder by spot and with the mouse, his bowls and `refill`, hunger and thirst worked out from the bowls, his litter tray and `scoop`, six breeds and the tortoiseshell and colourpoint coats, two cats together, 51 clips, 61 CC0 recordings, a voice of his own and his mouth moving to it); the other needs, carers and the other pet verbs are design.** The statue it started from is stowed (see [Scrappy](#scrappy)).

A pet is an animal that lives in your home. It's in the text world as an NPC you can see, hear and talk to, and in ROOM as an animal walking about your flat. For now it never leaves the home it was given to. You feed it, pet it, play with it, brush it and clean up after it, and it does a lot of things on its own while you watch.

The first pet is a cat, and the first cat is Scrappy. Cats will be customizable, but Scrappy gets everything first: the model, the behaviours, eating, sleeping, petting and the sounds are all built and tuned on him, and only then opened up to other cats. The aim is the best cat in any game: a cat that does what cats actually do, for the reasons cats actually do it.

## Scrappy

Scrappy was a marble statue in Solenne 30-B (`furn_solenne_b_fountain`, the `catstatue` form in `gl/room-decor.js`, carved by `gl/cat-statue.js`). The statue is stowed: the furniture file is out of `content/` and kept beside this doc as `pets/furn_solenne_b_fountain.json`, and the carving code stays where it is. Put the file back in `content/furniture/` to put him back.

The statue is where the live cat starts. It was one signed distance field (ellipsoids and tapered limbs joined by smooth unions, the details cut in) meshed once. The cat kit (`client/game/js/panels/gl/cat-kit.js`) is the same field hung on a skeleton: every piece belongs to a bone and is written where it sits on a cat standing square, and a pose moves the bones, so a folded hip is carved folded and blends into the flank the way the stone did. The head, the ears, the eyes, the lids and the paws are the statue's own shapes. Each pose is baked once (about 1.5 s at 2.7 mm); what moves while a pose is held (breathing, the head looking about, each ear on its own, the tail, a slow blink) is that bake skinned to the bones, and the eyes, lids and whiskers are built every frame on the head.

The body and legs are built on a real cat's skeleton and muscles (sources in [bibliography.md](../reference/bibliography.md#cats)). Each leg's joints sit where a standing cat's do: the shoulder joint at the point of the chest, the elbow at the bottom of the ribs, the hip high under the croup, the stifle high in the flank and the hock high, with the long metatarsus upright under it. Each muscle group is an ellipsoid laid along its bone (`mkAxis`): the shoulder blades flat on a rib cage that narrows to the front, the triceps filling the angle behind the upper arm, the forearm's extensors and flexors running down into a slender wrist with its carpal pad and dewclaw, the biceps femoris and hamstrings fanning from the point of the buttock, the calf running into the Achilles tendon. The side view is checked against a 19th-century engraving of the skeleton inside its outline. Each paw is a round mitten with four toes, each its own lobe under a raised knuckle; the coat darkens down the partings between them and the fur parts there, so the toes read from the front, and the pad colour is only on the sole, where the skin faces the floor. A leg asked to reach further than it can falls short on a straight leg rather than leaving its paw behind.

He's off two photos of him: a fat, fluffy tom with a black saddle over his back and sides from behind the shoulders to the tail, a white chest, belly and front legs, black hind legs in white socks, and a black patch on the front of his left shoulder. On his face: a thin black cap joining the bases of his ears, a patch round the outer side of his left eye that runs up into a black left ear (the right ear is white, pink inside), a handlebar moustache, bigger on his right, a spot on his chin, a pink nose and pale round eyes. In one photo he's sitting up on his bum like a person, front paws in the air, which goes on the list of idles.

He's a tuxedo American Shorthair (`breed: 'american_shorthair'`, `coat.pattern: 'tuxedo'`) and a full-grown tom: about 47 cm nose to rump (`scale` 0.7, the breed's 0.66 and an ordinary cat's 0.6), a big round head with a tom's jowls (`jowls`), a high round skull (`dome`), a short muzzle, ears small for the head and rounded at the tips (`earRound`), large round pale eyes, and a dense, plush coat longer than the breed's flat one. His own marks are laid over the tuxedo's white.

The Cattery (`/cattery.html` on the Modelshop, recipes in `tools/modelshop/cattery-recipes.js`) shows him and three other cats in four poses (stand, sit, loaf and curl), idling, with sliders for the build, the coat and the eyes and a random-cat button. `scripts/cats/cat-smoke.mjs` is the gate.

The face is carved on a grid twice as fine as the body's (`faceH`), inside a ball round the head, with the seam in the ruff; that's most of the 240k triangles. It follows how a cat's face is built: the eyes on the head's midline, about an eye's width apart, slanting up and out under full upper lids with a dark rim, and a dark line at the inner corner; a small inverted-triangle nose with a nostril either side, set lower than you'd think (measured off a photograph from the front, the eye line to the tip of the nose is about 1.2 times the tip of the nose to the chin: `NY` and `PY` in the cat kit); puffy whisker pads as their own volume, with four rows of roots and twelve whiskers on each, two over each eye and two on the chin; the head widest at the cheeks under the eyes. The ear is a cone of cartilage opened at the front, its inner edge straight and its outer edge bowed, the cup deepest where the canal turns down into the head, a fold across it, rolled rims, Henry's pocket in the outer edge at the root, and pale furnishings fanning out of it on the ear's own bone. A patch that needs a designed edge (his left-eye patch) is a polygon traced off the photos in the face's front view (a `poly` mark) rather than an ellipse.

Parts that are never joined (one leg and another, the tail and a leg, a forearm or a shin and the body, a paw and the head) still touch in a pose: a sitting cat's tail wraps round its forepaws and its forearms hang against its chest. Carved as one field they fused into one skin, so when a clip parted them the skin stretched in a web (a Maine Coon up boxing dragged its tail tip off the floor by its paw). Two things keep them apart. The sit is measured for the build (`sitClear`): it puts the forepaws forward and the elbows out a step at a time until each forearm stands clear of the chest below the elbow, and widens the tail's loop until it lies clear of the legs, as a big cat really sits (the statue's sit had every cat's forearm 2.5 to 4 cm into its chest, and a Maine Coon's tail round its forepaw). And the carve cuts a seam two and a half cells wide down the plane between any two such parts that still touch, out as far as the body's smooth blends reach (a forearm 3 cm off the belly was still webbed to it by the blend's fillet): a forearm or shin parts from the body except right at the elbow or stifle (`JOIN_R`, under the fur), the seam fading in over `JOIN_FADE` so it leaves no notch there. A limb's skin follows only its own bones, with only a leg's top bone, the tail's root and the skin round the elbow and stifle blending into the body. A folded shin isn't cut from its own thigh: that printed the shin's outline on every sitting cat's thigh and parted nothing a clip needed. Measured on a Maine Coon boxing, faces stretched past 3 cm went from 734 to 51, all where the tail really joins the rump.

The face is lit through a rounder normal than the one it's carved with (`faceSoft` on the bake, 0.6 by default): the field's normal bent toward the way out from a point set back in the skull, fading out toward the ruff, so the comic terminator crosses the head as one ball instead of cutting a band along the cheeks and whisker pads; the creases still read from the shading baked into the coat. A pose is carved in a worker (`bakeCatOff` in `gl/cat-bake.js`, the worker `gl/cat-bake-worker.js`), which hands back the same bake `bakeCat` would, so a room or the Cattery keeps drawing while it carves.

Still rough: the rounder face light hasn't been seen on a real GPU yet; the curl reads as a sleeping cat from above but has no weight where it meets the floor; the fur shells and the GPU skinning have only been seen under software GL (SwiftShader), so they need a look on a real GPU; there's no jaw, so the yawn and the meows show on the head and ears but not the mouth; a tail raised straight up shows faint ridges at its joints; and a hind paw folded under a crouch sinks up to 3 cm into the floor, where the floor hides it.

### Moving (phase 3, started)

**On the GPU.** Skinning a carve on the CPU (`skinCat`) costs 60 to 130 ms, so in ROOM each carve is uploaded once with every vertex's bones and weights (`gl/cat-gpu.js`), and only the bones go up each frame: up to 40 rows of 3×4 (`catBoneRows`), from where each bone was carved to where it is now, in metres and in the room. The body is indexed; the fur is the same vertices drawn again, instanced, one instance a shell. The room's ink, depth and beast shaders take the skin through `skinVariant`, which renames their inputs and calls `catSkin()` first, so the cat is lit and inked as the beasts are. The eyes, lids, whiskers and ear tufts are four small buffers on the head and ear bones, each built again only when its own number moves, so a blink rebuilds the lids alone; the eyes go through many values (the pupil and where they look), so the drawer keeps the 48 it used last. The coat's shells are drawn with alpha-to-coverage (`uFurSoft` in gl/beast-light.js): a strand covers part of a pixel at its edge, and where strands are smaller than a pixel the coat goes to a haze rather than a speckle. Nothing in that path is discarded, because a discard left the comic light's derivatives undefined for the pixels round it and they came out lit in the shadow. The room gives the long fur about six layers over all of it (a budget of 480k shell triangles on a desktop, 60k on a phone), and the ink is pushed out through most of the fur's length (`uCatFurInk`), so the line goes round the outside of the coat. The whiskers and ear furnishings are smooth curves of crossed ribbons, so they never go edge-on and vanish.

**Clips** (`gl/cat-clips.js`) are poses over time, each carved from one of the four bakes: stand, sit, loaf and sleep; walk, trot and gallop; groom (lick a forepaw, wipe it over the ear), flank, scratch (a hind foot behind the ear), yawn, stretch (front, then each back leg), knead, sit up like a person, flop on his side, sniff, watch (with the chatter), claw the rug, stalk and pounce, rub round somebody's legs, wait looking up, petted, cross, and the night yowl; the cello (sat back washing the base of a hind leg held straight up beside his head), a shake of the head, the slow blink, rolling on his back, dreaming (paws paddling and whiskers going in his sleep), a hairball, rubbing his cheek on the corner of the furniture (`mark`), demanding food, and the tray's two (`pee` and `poo`: a dig, a turn round, the squat, a sniff and a long cover); and with another cat, touching noses, standing to be sniffed, sniffing its back end, grooming it, being groomed, boxing, hissing and cowering. A gait's paws slide back under the body at its speed while they're down, so the cat moves across the floor at the same speed. The Cattery plays any of them (its clip list, or `?clip=groom`).

**His day** (`client/shared/cat-life.js`) is worked out from his id and the clock, the same on the server and every client: four-minute blocks of a state (asleep, resting, sat up, about, flopped out), each cut into 20-second turns that pick what he does in it. Each block mostly carries on the last (`STAY`), and only when it doesn't does the hour choose what's next (more sleep in the small hours, more going about at dawn and dusk); the chain starts afresh every three hours, so it stays a pure function of the clock. Measured over days he sleeps 13 to 14 hours, in bouts of about an hour, as a cat does; with each block drawn on its own he'd slept 7.5 hours in bouts of 7 minutes. The first turn after sleep is a stretch. Asleep he dreams about one turn in five; sat, he now and then does the cello, shakes his head or gives the room a slow blink (more often the more outgoing he is); about, he rubs his cheek on the corner of something (`catMarkOf` picks the piece and the corner, the same for the words and the picture) and about one day in two brings up a hairball; flopped out, he rolls about on his back. Each block has its own spot, and while he's about he wanders to new ones, so he walks across the room between them, round the furniture (`roomPath`).

**Up on things** (`catPerchOf`, cat-life.js). A settled cat (asleep, resting, sat, flopped out) is up on the furniture about half the time: the bed, the sofa, a chair, a table, the kitchen counter, the top of a cupboard. Which piece is picked from the room's layout and the block's spot, so the server's words and every client's picture agree; how high he can jump goes down as he gets heavier (`catLeap`: Scrappy clears 1.1 m, so not the fridge). Each kind of piece weighs the same however many there are, so six dining chairs don't outvote the bed. A settled cat keeps its spot for as long as it stays settled the same way, so a long sleep is one place. In ROOM he walks to the floor beside the piece, crouches with his eyes on the top and his bum wiggling, jumps, and lands a little in from its edge; down is front end first. A chair or a sofa knows where its back is (`face` and `backTop` on the perch): he goes up and comes down by the front or a side, settles clear of the back, and if behind it is the only way, the jump arcs over the top of the back rather than through it. He kneads only on something soft (`catSettle`): a bed, a sofa, a cushioned chair, or the rug, which a settled cat on the floor mostly lies on; on a hard top he loafs instead.

**His day in words.** plugins/pets says what he does as it changes, every 5 seconds while somebody's in the room to hear it: settling, getting up, jumping up onto the bed or down off the counter, going to his bowls, and now and then what he does in a turn (a wash, a scratch, a yawn, a stare, the biscuits), never the same act twice running and no more than one every 75 seconds. The people's routines (ambient-life's home-life and intrusion, the Room Stage's residents) skip an NPC with `flags.animal`, so nothing sends him to the bathroom or cooks him dinner. Until that rule the text had him using the en-suite while the 3D cat sat still.

**His bowls.** A feeding station is a piece of furniture with `flags.pet_bowls` naming the pet (30-B's is at the closed end of the galley kitchen), drawn as the `petbowl` form: a rubber mat, a steel dish of biscuits and a steel bowl of water, each drawn at its level. The dish holds two meals and the bowl a day's water; he eats at about 07:40 and 18:40 and drinks four times, in real time (the game's clock can run fast; a cat's stomach doesn't), nudged a little per cat and per day (`bowlTimes`). A bowl's whole state is when it was last filled, a `pet_bowls` row, so how full it is (`bowlLevel`) and whether he's at it (`catAtBowls`) are worked out the same everywhere and nothing is written while he eats. At an empty one he sits by it and complains. `refill` (or `refill water`, `refill food`) fills what's gone, ₵6 for a whole dish of biscuits and ₵1 for a bowl of water, the share that's missing and at least ₵1, taken there and then; if he'd been left waiting he's at it before the rattle stops. `examine` says how full they are, and the piece carries a Refill button in ROOM.

**Hunger and thirst** (`catNeeds`, cat-life.js) are worked out when they're read, from the one thing anybody does about them: filling his bowls. When he last ate is the last of his goes since the dish was filled that found something in it (`lastFed`; with no go since, the one before the fill). Hunger is nothing for four hours after a meal and rises to 1 over the next twenty, so on an ordinary day he's a little peckish by his next meal (about 0.5 at worst) and a day without food has him starving; thirst does the same from three hours over fifteen. Nothing ticks, and nothing is stored but the bowls' own row. Hungry (0.4) at an empty dish, he goes back to it between meals to complain, once in each 35 minutes at a time hashed off him (a `pester` go in `catAtBowls`, so the server's words and every client agree), sitting by it demanding (`demand`) and looking round at whoever's in the room; he greets whoever comes in by demanding food whatever he was doing (the greet beat carries `hungry`) and `examine` says how he is (the `npc.describeAnimal` hook). Thirsty (0.35) at an empty water bowl he does the same every 45 minutes, and half of those goes he jumps up onto the kitchen sink and drinks from the drip of the tap instead (the sink is a top in `catPerches` with no weight for settling, so it's only ever where he goes to drink). A full dish he just eats.

**His tray.** A litter tray is a piece of furniture with `flags.pet_tray` naming the pet (30-B's is in the en-suite, in the corner by the door), drawn as the `littertray` form: a grey plastic tray with a high back, litter, the clumps and the other half dug into it, and a scoop leant on the back. He pees three or four times a day and does the other once, most often an hour or two after breakfast, and some days again in the evening, in real time and nudged per cat and per day (`trayTimes`), about five goes a day. The tray's whole state is when it was last scooped (a `pet_trays` row), so every go since, and how dirty it is (`trayState`), is worked out from that and nothing is written while he goes. Past four goes he sniffs it over before he gets in; at seven it's full and he won't, and goes on the floor in front of it instead, which the tray draws beside itself and the room and the en-suite smell of (`zone.smells`). `scoop` empties it and cleans the floor, free. In ROOM he walks there (through the en-suite door), hops in over the rim (a `step` jump, no measuring it), digs facing the back, turns round to face out, squats staring at nothing, sniffs and covers (`LITTER` in gl/cat-clips.js times the phases from when he got there on that client), then hops out; after the other, a bit under half the time, he bolts and tears round the flat for a few seconds, as cats do.

**How he moves.** Nothing is tweened. Every layer of the body follows what it's after through second-order dynamics (`client/shared/second-order.js`, after t3ssel8r: frequency f, damping ζ, response r), with the numbers set from his nature (`motionOf` in gl/room-beasts.js): his heading, his speed (he gets going and brakes), his head turning to look at somebody, his ears and their tips, his lean back on his haunches as he brakes and into a turn, and the dip and bounce when he lands a jump. An impulsive cat turns quick and overshoots; a skittish one's head and ears snap round ahead of the rest of him. Under that, breath, gait and the look about are harmonics. His paws go to the ground under each one (a stair, the edge of the top he's on) by the legs' two-bone reach, and the body pitches with the difference. Now and then, by his attention and the clock, he turns his head to look at somebody in the room.

**His tail** (`gl/cat-tail.js`) is a spring chain in the room: the pose says where he means it to be (`tailTarget`), and each point is a mass on a spring to there, stiff at the root and loose at the tip, under gravity scaled by how much he's carrying it (a sleeping cat lets it lie), held to its length, and kept on top of whatever's under it. It trails when he sets off, swings on when he stops, counterbalances a jump, and hangs over the edge of a counter.

**His eyes.** The pupils follow the light: the room passes how much there is where he is (the lamps, and the day through the windows if it has any), and they go from a slit with pointed ends in sunlight to a full circle in the dark, closing down quicker than they open; each cat's own pupil tilts it a little, and excitement opens them wider. Where he looks, the eyes get there first: what the head hasn't turned yet the eyes make up, so on a new target they flick across and come back to the middle as the head catches up, and something close (a hand) turns them both in. With nothing to look at they make small shifts of their own. The iris is a cat's: a pale jagged ring round the pupil, fine fibres out from it, darker pits through its middle, a dark ring at its edge and the upper lid's shadow across its top, seeded by the coat.

**His ears** move through the CatFACS ear actions (`EAR_MOODS`, `earsFor`): forward, pulled together, flattened sideways (airplane ears), rotated so their backs face you, and drooped, with neutral, sleepy, content and listening (one ear each way). Each ear has a tip bone, so a flat ear folds over and the tip swings on after the ear has turned. The clips use the moods: rotated when he swats, together when he watches a bird or stalks, forward when he waits on you, airplane through a yawn, content while he's stroked, sleepy when he sleeps.

**Stroking him.** Press on him in ROOM and drag. What he does depends on the spot under the pointer (`catHit`): under the chin his head tips right back; on a cheek he rubs his face along your hand one way and the other; on the head he pushes up into it; down the back he arches up with his tail straight up; on the tail or a paw he turns round, ears out sideways, tail lashing; the belly is a trap. He answers at once on your screen, purring, with a contented trill or meow now and then, and about once a second the stroke goes to the server as the typed `pet scrappy under the chin`, so his ladder runs and everybody sees him answer. The ladder is points that fade (a half-life of 36 s), each spot weighing what it is to a cat: a long scratch under the chin before he's had enough, a shorter stroke down the back, a couple of goes at the tail. Hovering over his face, he looks at the pointer. He's stroked in whatever he was doing when the hand came (`petted`, `pettedLoaf`, `pettedStand`, `pettedSleep`, `archLoaf`), so a hand on a lying cat doesn't sit him up, and asleep he barely wakes. His head wanders on slow drifts, not a fixed sway, the lids squint and open with a slow blink now and then, and every so often he turns his face off to one side and holds it there. While it's nice he mostly doesn't look at you: a glance at the hand when it first comes, then only now and then, more for an outgoing cat. Going from one clip to the next, or from one spot under the hand to another, the frame he was on blends into the new one over half a second.

**Breeds** (`gl/cat-breeds.js`). A breed is a set of the kit's numbers a cat starts from, and a cat is its breed with its own numbers over them (`catLook({ breed, ... })`); a coat pattern (`coat.pattern`: solid, locket, mitted, tuxedo, bicolour, harlequin, van) sets how far the white goes, and a coat colour (`coat.colour`) sets the coats that are more than one colour: tortie, blue-cream, calico, dilute calico, and seal, chocolate, blue and lilac points. The kit's own numbers are an ordinary domestic shorthair. The breeds, from the CFA standards: the American Shorthair; the Siamese, the traditional cat people keep rather than the show ring's extreme wedge (a rounded wedge of a head, ears about as tall as from the eye to the chin, pale blue almond eyes slanting toward the nose, a colourpoint with a smoky mask, a paler chin and a fawn back); the Maine Coon (big and long, a mane, lynx-tipped ears full of furnishings, a plume of a tail); the Persian (cobby, a round flat face, small ears low and wide, copper eyes, the whole coat standing off it); the British Shorthair (round, grey, copper-eyed, a dense plush coat); and the Abyssinian (lithe, big-eared, every hair ticked). The Cattery has one of each, and a tortie, a torbie and a calico.

The coat's genes beyond white spotting and the tabby: `points` darkens the cool extremities continuously (the ears, the tail more toward the tip, the legs from the elbow and stifle down, and the mask, darkest on the nose and rising to a soft point up the forehead, the chin left paler), with `shade` the darkening along an older pointed cat's back and crown; `tortie` lays the red over the black in soft patches flecked together at their edges (`brindle`), each patch keeping its tabby stripes for a torbie; `grain` is ticking, a fine light and dark speckle through the coat; and the tabby has `ticked` (no stripes on the body or legs, only the face's lines, a line down the spine and a dark tail tip) and `rosette` kinds. The build has `snout` (how narrow the muzzle and whisker pads are), `ruff` (the mane carved round the neck and down the chest), `bodyFluff` (a long coat's barrel), `earSet` (low and wide, or high on the crown), `furnish` (the length of the hair out of the ears) and `lynx` (the tufts off their tips, on the ear's tip bone, so they fold with it); the eyes have `slant`.

**Two cats** (`catMeets` in cat-life.js, staged by `gl/cat-meet.js`). Cats that share a home get on, or don't, in the ways the studies of house cats describe (Crowell-Davis et al. 2004; van den Bos 1998): a greeting (nose to nose, a sniff round the back end while the other looks round at it, then a rub of heads as they pass), grooming (lying alongside, the groomer washing the other's head and neck; the groomer is the bossier of the two), sleeping in a heap (curled up against each other for the whole of the sleep, on the floor or up on the piece one of them was on if there's room for two), play (up on their hind legs boxing, then a chase, and again) and a spat (the bossier one side-on with its back arched, ears flat, hissing; the other flat on the floor with its head turned away, then out of it). Which happens, between whom and when is worked out from the two ids, their natures and the clock, the same on the server and every client, so it costs no messages: a turn or two at a time for the awake ones, and as long as both sleeps last for a heap. How well two cats get on (`catBond`) is their friendliness, less how bossy the less bossy one is and how nervy the nervier one is. Over a simulated day a friendly pair greet about 30 times, groom for 25 minutes, play a dozen times at dawn and dusk and sleep in a heap for over two hours; a pair that doesn't get on greets stiffly now and then and has about a dozen spats, and never grooms or shares a bed. One cat stays put (its own day says where) and the other comes to it; the phases' clock starts when the one coming gets to its place. plugins/pets says each encounter once as it starts, and neither cat's own day while it runs. A cat at its bowls or on a beat is left out. The Cattery plays any of them with any two cats (`?meet=greet&with=siamese`).

**Beats** from the server go over the day (plugins/pets): `greet` when somebody comes in (he trots to the door they came by, or to them, rubs round their legs, then sits looking up, waiting), `petted` while he's stroked (with the spot, so everybody sees the same answer), and `cross` when somebody kept going. Where he walks is each client's own; what he's doing is the same for everybody.

The model bar, in the comic look ([comic-ink.md](comic-ink.md)):

- A cat's body, not a small dog's: a long, loose back that bends a lot, shoulder blades that ride up when he stalks, a primordial pouch that swings when he trots, hind legs longer than the front.
- A head that moves on its own: ears that each turn and flatten separately (about 30 muscles each), whiskers that fan forward when he's curious and lie back when he's scared, pupils from slits to full circles with the light and his mood, a third eyelid on a slow blink.
- A tail with its own spine of about 20 joints, driven by mood, not by the gait.
- Fur as shells or cards along the outline, so a puffed tail and raised hackles actually change his outline.

### A cat is a set of numbers

Scrappy is built as one set of parameters from the start, so a second cat is a second set and not a second model (`CAT` and `catLook` in the cat kit): the build (`length`, `neckLen`, `belly`, `pouch`, `chest`, `hips`, `neck`, `legLen`, `legW`, `head`, `cheeks`, `ears`, `muzzle`, `tailLen`, `tailW`, and a long coat's `tailFluff` and `legFluff`), the coat (a base colour, white spotting from none to van, tabby stripes, and marks laid over it on the body or the head), the eyes (iris, rim, pupil, and `size`, `open`, `sunk` and `droop`, which shape the eye, its socket and how far the upper lid rests down) and the fur lengths. A long coat stands out further than the fur shells can carry (past about 1.2 cm they show their layers), so `tailFluff` carves the tail as a plume, full for most of its length, and `legFluff` carves the coat's volume onto the legs: fuller lower legs, feathering down the back of the forearm, a skirt behind each hind leg and a tuft at the heel. Personality joins it in phase 2. Nothing in the model, the clips or the sim may mention Scrappy by name; he's a row of data like any other cat will be. Customization itself (a picker, more patterns) waits until he's finished.

## The cat sim

Everything he does comes from a few numbers, as Cathode's behaviour comes from `moodToward()` ([systems-strays.md](../systems-strays.md)).

### Needs

Each need is a level and a timestamp, worked out when it's read, never on a tick. Hunger, thirst and the litter are built ([His bowls](#moving-phase-3-started), above); the rest are design.

| Need | Rises with | Met by |
|---|---|---|
| hunger ✅ | time since his last meal from a filled dish | a dish that's been filled (`refill`) |
| thirst ✅ | time since he last drank from a filled bowl | a water bowl; failing that, the kitchen tap |
| tiredness | being awake; a cat sleeps 12 to 16 hours a day | sleeping in his spot, which moves with the sun |
| boredom | time indoors | `play`, a toy, a window with birds |
| litter ✅ | his own times, about five goes a day | a clean tray (`scoop`); a full one and he goes in front of it |
| grooming | time, mess | grooming himself; `brush` for the bits he can't reach |

### Bond and personality

Bond is how much he trusts you, per person, and it moves slowly. Personality is fixed per cat, on the five axes of the Feline Five (Litchfield et al., 2017): skittish, outgoing, dominant, impulsive and friendly. Scrappy is outgoing, a bit dominant, not skittish. Two cats with the same needs and the same bond do different things.

### What he does on his own

Each is an idle clip in ROOM and a line in the text world. The clip is picked by the server from the needs, the bond, the time of day and the room; the client only plays it.

- Sleeping: curled tight when cold, belly up when warm and safe, loafed with paws tucked, in a sunbeam that moves through the day.
- Grooming: paw licked then wiped over the ear, a hind leg up, a sudden bite at an itch.
- Greeting: tail straight up with a hooked tip, a trot over, a head bunt or a rub along your shins.
- Kneading on a soft thing, purring, eyes half shut.
- The slow blink, which you can return (`blink at`), and which raises bond.
- Zoomies at dusk and dawn, when cats are most active, with a skid on the turn.
- Watching the window, with the chatter at birds. The birds are the real birds outside ([systems-fauna.md](../systems-fauna.md)), in the window view ROOM already paints.
- Stalking a toy or a moth: low, bum wiggle, pounce.
- Knocking a small thing off a table while looking at you.
- Sitting in any box or bag left on the floor, and on whatever you're reading.
- Hiding under the bed when there are strangers, loud noise or he's ill.
- Bringing you a gift.
- Yawning, stretching (front down, then back legs), the flehmen face at a smell.

### Petting

He decides how long petting stays nice. The head, cheeks and chin are safe; the back is fine for a while; the belly is a trap. As petting goes on he shows it's enough, in order: a twitching tail tip, skin rippling on his back, ears turned sideways, a look, then a bite or a swipe. A player who stops at the first sign raises bond; one who ignores them loses it. This is the same refusal ladder Cathode has, run in seconds instead of visits.

Built so far, without bond: inside 90 seconds the first two strokes are welcome, the third twitches his tail, the fourth gets the ears and a look, and the fifth a growl and a swat before he walks off (the `cross` clip). Where you stroke him is still to do.

### Talking

Cats meow mostly at people, not at each other. He meows to be fed, to be let somewhere, to say hello, and each sounds different. He also trills, purrs, chirps at birds, hisses and growls.

These are recordings, not SIREN cues: 61 CC0 cat recordings from Freesound (`cat_*` in [field-recordings.js](../../client/shared/field-recordings.js), credited in [bibliography.md](../reference/bibliography.md#field-recordings)), built by `scripts/audio/build-field-recordings.mjs` like the birds. Each clip names its sound (`sound` in gl/cat-clips.js): a bed under it (a purr, the snore, the licking of a groom) and calls at moments (a meow while he waits, the chatter at a bird, a growl when he's had enough). [room-cat-sound.js](../../client/game/js/panels/room-cat-sound.js) plays them where he is, and picks which call by hashing the cat, the clip and the loop, so everybody in the room hears the same meow. The bowls play the dry-food crunch and the lapping, and the tray the scratch of litter while he digs and covers.

Every cat has his own voice (`voiceOf` in room-cat-sound.js), worked out from his id: two meows of his own out of the twenty adult ones (one cat in five squeaks, with a kitten's), his own trill, chirp, yowl, hiss and purr, and a pitch of his own, lower for a bigger breed. A clip asks for a kind of call and the cat answers in his voice; the cross and grumpy meows stay as the clip asks.

His mouth moves with what he says. While a call plays, its loudness at that moment, read off the decoded recording (`AudioEngine.sampleEnvelope`), opens his mouth as far as that kind of call does: wide for a meow, a yowl or a hiss, a little for a chirp, hardly at all for a trill or a growl, and shut for a purr, as a cat purrs. The open mouth is `mo.mouth` (0 to 1), drawn on the head the way the lids are (the `mouth` part of `catBits`, the dark of the mouth with the tongue at its floor and the upper fangs), one mesh a tenth of the way open. The Cattery holds it open with `?mouth=1`, or works it with `?mouth=talk`.

### Neglect

He doesn't die of neglect. A hungry cat yells, then sulks, then hides, and the bond drops. Built so far: a hungry cat yells at his dish and at whoever comes in, and a full tray means a mess on the floor in front of it and a smell, until somebody scoops it. The sulking, the hiding and the bond are design. After long enough with nobody caring for him, he's gone: rehomed, with a note.

## How it's built

- **Plugin `plugins/pets/`.** Verbs: `feed`, `pet`, `play`, `brush`, `call`, `name`, `blink`, `pick up`, `scoop` (built, with `refill`; `clean` is the cleaning plugin's). It owns the pet and nothing else's verbs.
- **State.** A `pets` table: id, species, name, home zone (null for a street cat), the body parameters, personality, and a JSONB of needs and timestamps. A `pet_carers` table: pet, player, bond, and when they last met. Loaded into RAM with the home; writes coalesced and flushed. No column on `players` or `npcs`.
- **The text world.** The pet shows in the room as an NPC. Its ambient lines come from the same state as the clip, so the text and the picture agree.
- **ROOM.** A `pet` actor in the Room Stage. The server sends a beat (what he's doing, where, from when); the client plays the clip and moves him between spots. Anything that's just a pose (a tail flick, an ear turn) is worked out on the client from the beat and the clock, as birds are, so it costs no messages.
- **The Modelshop.** The cat is a subject there with a pose lab, so every clip can be looked at without a server.

## Phases

Phases 1 to 4 are all Scrappy. Phases 5 and 6 open it up.

1. ✅ The model: Scrappy carved from the statue's field on a skeleton, from his parameter set, in the Modelshop with stand, sit, loaf and curl, idling.
2. The plugin: the table, the needs, `feed`/`pet`/`name`, Scrappy in the text world in Cyd's home. Built so far: `plugins/pets`, the `pets` table (`content/pets/`), Scrappy as `npc_pet_scrappy` in Solenne 30-B, and the Room Stage drawing him as the cat (`roomstage.npcBeast`, `gl/room-beasts.js`), moving all day (phase 3, below), greeting whoever comes in and claiming `pet` for the ladder. The needs and the other verbs are still to do.
3. ROOM: the actor, walking between spots, eating from his bowl, sleeping and grooming, petting with the ladder. Built so far: skinning on the GPU, the clips, his day, walking round the furniture between spots, the greeting at the door, the ladder, his bowls, hunger and thirst, and his tray (see [Moving](#moving-phase-3-started)).
4. The rest of the clips, the sounds, play and toys, the window and the birds. Built so far: 51 clips and 38 recordings. Play, toys and the real birds at the window are still to do.
5. Customizable cats: coats, builds and personalities from the same parameters, and a picker.
6. Street cats and the shelter: cats with no home on the streets, a shelter to adopt from, and taking a street cat in.

## Decided

- **Scrappy is Cyd's.** He lives in Cyd's home, and that's where he's built and tested.
- **Everything is a foundation.** Every piece built for Scrappy (the model, the clips, the needs, the verbs, the table) has to work for any player with any cat. Scrappy is the first row, never a special case. A check that only passes because the cat is Scrappy, or the owner is Cyd, is a bug.
- **Where cats come from.** Cats live on the streets, and there's a cat shelter. A player can take in a street cat or adopt one from the shelter. Street cats use the same model, clips and personality as pets; a pet is a cat with a home and an owner, and a street cat is one without. Cathode ([systems-strays.md](../systems-strays.md)) should end up drawn and animated the same way.
- **A pet stays where it's set.** A pet belongs to whoever has its flat, player or NPC. The boot sweep that moves NPCs out of player-owned flats (`npcHomedInOwnedUnit` in `server/engine/apartments.js`) skips anything with `flags.pet`, and `evict` refuses one. Until that rule, the sweep moved Scrappy out of Cyd's 30-B at every boot.
- **A cat has carers, not one owner.** Anyone who lives in the cat's home can feed, pet and care for it, and the cat keeps a separate bond with each of them. So the table has one row per cat and one row per carer, never an owner column on the cat that a second person has to be squeezed into.
- **A cat can change hands.** A pet can be given to another player or let go onto the street, and a street cat can be taken in. Going from pet to street cat and back only changes the cat's home and carers; its body, personality and memory of each person go with it.
