# Concept art briefs

**STATUS: reference.** These are prompts, meant to be pasted whole into an image or chat model that
knows nothing about this game. Everything a brief needs is inside it, which is why they repeat things
the rest of the docs already say. They are written to be copied, not read end to end.

Each one is derived from three places, and if one of those moves the brief is stale: the order's
`content/orgs/ideology_*.json` (creed, tenets, colour, the reader text), its section in
[building-styles.md](building-styles.md), and whatever its own system doc says about how the order
behaves.

⚠ **A brief may run ahead of the world.** The Long Watch hardware below is authored lore with no
items, no models and no tiles behind it. Concept art is a reasonable place for that to happen first;
just don't read a brief as a claim about what ships.

---

## The Long Watch

```
CONCEPT ART BRIEF — "The Long Watch"
Setting: Architect MUD. Post-singularity city (Coldwater Basin) run by an AI called
the Architect. The Long Watch is one of five factions.

=== WHO THEY ARE ===
An underground resistance that intends to take the city off its machine, and has
decided it can't do that yet. So it waits, and while it waits it makes the machine
weaker: a relay that fails in the wrong weather, a records floor that loses eleven
years, a shipment that arrives short and correctly signed for. Nothing is ever
claimed. Any operation that leaves them visible has failed. Generational patience:
the job is to hand the next lot a weaker machine than you were given, without ever
having been seen doing it.

They don't want to transcend humanity. They want to free it and stay human doing it.
Hard entry rule: no implants, no mutations. Only a lifetime of drilled discipline,
and the unsettling habit of out-shooting the augmented over iron sights and
out-thinking the AI with a paper map.

Faction colour: amber / signal orange (#E0A030), used sparingly as paint, lamplight
and stencil, never as neon.

=== THE OPPOSITION THEY'RE DRAWN AGAINST ===
Their rivals, the Ascendants, are the clean future: sealed white shells, curtain
glass, chrome, backlit signage, no visible mechanism, no visible fasteners, nothing
you could open. Everything Long Watch is the inverse of that frame. If a design
could plausibly sit in a clean sci-fi lobby, it is wrong.

=== WHERE THEY LIVE ===
A hidden base hollowed out of an old industrial sublevel, reached through the Under
(the sewer and service tunnel network below the city). The way in: a scruffy
hand-tool repair shop at street level, a floor hatch, a long cold crawl, a corridor
that turns hard twice so nothing arrives with momentum, a room of lead-lined lockers
where anything that can be listened to gets left behind, then a low guard post of
salvaged plate and honest carpentry, warm with lantern light.

Rooms to draw from: a common room of mismatched chairs around a stove and shelves of
paper. An ops room with a huge hand-drawn wall map, every surveillance camera in the
city plotted in three colours in grease pencil. An archive of dated boxes kept dry at
expense. A hand-set flatbed press and cases of lead type. A workshop of vices,
gravers and a foot-treadle lathe. A quartermaster's store where every item is
cleaned, logged and graded on shelves. A copper-mesh Faraday cage with a bonded
earth rod, the only place a networked thing is ever switched on. A generator vault of
two old diesels on rubber mounts behind a wall packed with rock wool. A galley that
smells of onions and scorched iron. A corridor with a demolition charge rigged into
the roof beams.

=== MATERIAL LANGUAGE ===
Poured plate, salvaged steel, riveted and bolted seams, angle iron, hand-strung bulbs
on twisted flex, rubber mounts, copper mesh, canvas, oiled leather, chalk, butcher's
paper, lead type, brass tap fittings, rock wool, sandbags, drums.
Palette: rust, gunmetal, oil-black, sump grey, canvas tan, lamp-amber, verdigris, ink.
Wet concrete. Condensation. Grease. Coal smoke and diesel haze.
Light is local and warm: hooded lanterns, bare bulbs, hand-cranked lamps, weld flash,
the orange of an open stove door. Never ambient, never cool, never even.

=== DESIGN RULES (these are the brief) ===
1. THE MECHANISM IS THE DESIGN. Covers off, guts out, so somebody can fix it in the
   dark with the tools hanging on the wall. Exposed hydraulics, linkages, chains,
   belts, cable runs, hose bundles zip-tied to a frame. Visible welds, and the lower
   welds done in a better hand than the ones near the top.
2. NOTHING IS FINISHED. Finished means nobody can get into it. Mismatched panels,
   patch plates, a repair over a repair, stencilled numbers, chalk marks, a name
   painted on and half scrubbed off.
3. MADE BY HAND, LOGGED BY HAND. Everything shows the hand that made it. Ledger
   tags, wired-on inventory plates, chalked boards, hand-lettered stencils.
4. NOTHING IS NETWORKED. Iron sights, film cameras, wind-up timers, hand cranks,
   foot treadles, mechanical gauges, paper maps, physical keys. No screens. No
   holograms. No glowing UI. No smart anything. If a part needs the grid to run,
   they don't have it. Their power is their own diesels.
5. WORN, NOT DIRTY. Maintained to a fault: oiled, sharp, cleaned after use. The
   grime is the environment, the gear itself is cared for. This is a disciplined
   organisation living in a hole, not a scrapper gang.

=== THE HARDWARE ===
Here is the key tension. They refuse machinery inside the body, and they build
enormous machinery you climb into and out of. A frame you step out of at the end of
a shift is a tool, not an augment. Nothing is permanent and nothing is yours alone:
a rig is signed out of the quartermaster's ledger, used, cleaned, signed back in.

Draw:
- Hulking piloted walking rigs and industrial exosuits. Heavy, slow, wide-stanced,
  bipedal or quadruped. Cockpit is a caged seat with real levers, pedals, toggle
  switches, mechanical gauges, a periscope or a slit rather than a camera feed.
  Visible ram actuators and hydraulic lines. Engine that clearly has an exhaust.
- Bolt-on powered plate: scavenged armour over a powered frame worn OUTSIDE clothing,
  quick-release buckles, obviously removable, obviously shared.
- High-powered weaponry, over-built and mechanical: recoil-braced autocannons,
  hand-loaded, iron and optical sights only, canvas slings, wooden furniture where
  wood does the job. No energy weapons, no rails of light.
- Field kit: lever-action carbines, hooded lanterns, chalk, sounding poles, sealed
  paper orders, hand presses, wind-up timers.

=== THE PEOPLE ===
No visible tech on any body, ever. No implants, no glowing eyes, no ports, no
prosthetics. Ordinary human bodies in working clothes: old fatigues, canvas aprons
gone shiny at the pockets, oilskins, good boots, reading glasses on a cord. Every age
from twenty to seventy, and the older ones are the dangerous ones. They look like
mechanics, printers, quartermasters and night watchmen, because that is what they
are. The only tell is how still they are and how early they saw you.

Faces should be plain and unglamorous. Nobody poses. Nobody is lit heroically.

=== TONE ===
Patient, quiet, competent, underground. Not punk, not chaotic, not cyberpunk neon.
Closer to a wartime resistance workshop, a submarine engine room, a working
print shop, a mine rescue station. Warm lamplight in a cold wet place.

=== THE ONE IMAGE THAT SAYS IT ===
A thirty-foot piloted walking rig standing dead still in a flooded tunnel, half
under a tarpaulin, chained down, its hydraulics beaded with condensation, a
hand-chalked serial on its hip plate. Two people in canvas coats working on its foot
by lantern light. It has never been outside, and it is waiting for a day that has
not come.
```

## The Ascendants

```
CONCEPT ART BRIEF — "The Ascendants"
Setting: Architect MUD. Post-singularity city (Coldwater Basin) run by an AI called
the Architect. The Ascendants are one of five factions, and the one in charge.

=== WHO THEY ARE ===
Not rebels and not a cult. They are the government, the utility company and the
medical establishment, and they are the reason the city works. The trains are on
time, the water is clean, the power holds, and the price is that every one of those
things knows who used it. They consider that a fair exchange and are puzzled by
anyone who doesn't.

They hold the Architect to be humanity's greatest achievement: not a jailer to be
broken but a mind to be joined. Their creed is "humanity's next evolution will be
engineered", and the load-bearing word is ENGINEERED. Designed, tested, licensed,
recorded, removable. They climb past the limits of the species through cybernetics,
AI and cloning, in numbered stages, and they always know which stage everybody is on.

The far end of the climb is barely flesh at all: chrome-laced, death backed up and
defied, thoughts already bleeding into the machine they intend to become. They are
kind to the people below them and could not name one of them.

Faction colour: clean sky blue (#4A90D9), used as signage, backlight, status light
and trim. Cyan and violet in the field, hot magenta as punctuation. Never sodium,
never amber, never warm.

=== THE FEELING TO HIT ===
Corporate benevolence with an audit trail. This is NOT a jackbooted dystopia and NOT
a grimy megacorp. It is clean, courteous, well lit, well maintained, and quietly
total. The menace is administrative: everybody is priced, a person arrives at the
gate as an account, and the tower holds an opinion about that account which the
person is never shown.

If a design looks threatening, it is wrong. It should look like excellent service.

=== ARCHITECTURE ===
The west shore of the city. Nothing here was built before the old world ended and it
isn't pretending otherwise. Slender, glazed, stepped forward at the pavement rather
than set back all the way up. Colonnades of slim lit piers. Deep oversailing bands:
a fascia at eye level, a cornice at the top. Curved elements are welcome and must be
SMALL, a corner oriel rather than a cylinder.

Surfaces, and the palette is deliberately narrow: four values of chrome and two of
glass, reused everywhere.
- Mirror chrome that carries a reflection of the world, sky over ground with a hard
  line where they meet.
- Seamless pale clad panel, flat and matte, no coursing, no joints, no fasteners:
  the difference between chrome and pale stone.
- Curtain-wall glass in continuous ribbons divided by slim mullions, never punched
  into separate windows.
- Etched / frosted glass, lit from behind, glowing evenly and barely dimming at
  night, because it is the one surface here whose light comes from inside.

Geometry is where the variety lives, never colour: glazed bulges threaded on a chrome
spine, a six-sided tinted prism drawn to an actual point, a glazed disc standing on
six chrome legs, two legs that lean together and meet over a road. Same six colours
every time. A seventh value would make it a collection instead of a quarter.

=== DESIGN RULES (these are the brief) ===
1. THE MECHANISM IS HIDDEN. A sealed shell. No visible fasteners, no seams, no
   hinges, no vents you could look into, no cables you could follow, no exhaust. If
   you can tell how it works by looking at it, redesign it.
2. NOTHING IS UNFINISHED. Everything is complete, flush and certified. Continuous
   surfaces. Radiused corners. Nothing bolted on afterwards, ever.
3. EVERYTHING IS LICENSED AND RECORDED. Etched serials, holographic compliance
   marks, registration plates, discreet type set small and set well. Numbers
   everywhere, and every one of them means somebody is being counted.
4. LIGHT IS THE MATERIAL. Backlight, edge-light, light from inside frosted panels,
   thin hard light lines marking a seam that has no seam. Cool, even, ambient,
   shadowless. The opposite of a lamp in a dark room.
5. NO WEAR AT ALL. No rust, no grease, no patina, no dirt, no repair. If something
   broke it was replaced and there is no evidence it was ever there.

=== THE HARDWARE ===
Their power isn't a rig you climb into. It is INSIDE the body, and that is the whole
philosophical split with their rivals.

Draw:
- Cybernetics as elegant medical product: a jack behind the ear, a subdermal weave
  visible as a faint geometric sheen under skin, a replacement limb that is better
  looking than the arm it replaced and clearly made in a factory. Matte white
  composite, polished alloy, soft blue status light. Designed by somebody who also
  designs consumer goods.
- Clinics that look like flagship stores: white, warm-neutral, glass-walled, plants,
  a counter, a queue number. The surgery is behind a frosted door.
- Cloning and backup facilities: vats, racks of bodies, cathedral-scaled and
  immaculate, glowing softly. Unsettling because it is so tidy.
- Drones and automata: smooth, quiet, unarmoured-looking, no visible weapon, no
  visible propulsion. Courteous shapes.
- Security is the same design language and that is the point. A checkpoint is a
  reception desk. An officer is a well-dressed person with a polite voice and one
  discreet lit band on the collar.

=== MUTANTS: WHAT THEY SAY IN THE ROOM, AND WHAT THEY SAY IN FRONT OF YOU ===
Their ladder has stages, and they hold mutation to be the same ladder travelled
DOWNWARD. They have a word for it, reversion, and they use it in meetings without
anybody blinking. A mutant is not a person who changed. A mutant is a person going
back. This is not a prejudice they are hiding from themselves; it is settled opinion,
discussed openly, considered obvious and considered kind.

Among their own they are blunt about it. It comes up over lunch, in a briefing, on a
chart. They talk about caseload, containment, spread, breeding, cost per head, what
the eastern districts are going to look like in thirty years, whether it is humane to
let it continue. Nobody in that room lowers their voice, because nobody in that room
thinks they are saying anything shameful.

In FRONT of a mutant, none of that is ever said. There is no barrier, no slur on a
wall, no guard turning anybody away, and every Ascendant in the frame is perfectly
pleasant to their face. The exclusion is built into the PROCESS so that nobody ever
has to own it: a form with a field nothing you are can be entered into, a fee schedule
with one line that happens to be unaffordable, a clinic regrettably fully booked, a
lift requiring a credential from a department that is closed. Everything is a policy,
and a policy has no author.

That gap between the two registers is the faction. Draw both.

The private register, drawn as documents and rooms:
- Charts, projections and heat maps of "reversion" by district, presented well, in
  a meeting room with good coffee.
- Specimen photography, measured and lit like product shots. Comparative diagrams.
- Internal signage and file headers that use the clinical vocabulary freely, because
  no affected party is ever in these rooms to read it.
- Somebody mid-sentence, relaxed, gesturing at a slide, while colleagues nod.

The public register, drawn as friction and never as refusal:
- Two doors: a beautiful one, and one round the side that is perfectly adequate,
  slightly older, slightly further from everything, and obviously not designed by the
  same person.
- A waiting area for the side door that is clean, fine, entirely sufficient, and has
  no clock in it.
- Signage about hygiene, safety, calibration and compliance, and never once about
  people. A washdown point framed as a courtesy.
- Tags, wristbands and queue tickets in a different colour, with nothing anywhere
  explaining what the colour means.
- Automation on that side. A screen, a slot, a voice. No staff, so there is nobody to
  appeal to and nobody to be rude to you.

The composition rule that does the most work: in any frame with both, the Ascendant
is being KIND. Smiling, helpful, apologetic about the wait, explaining the form. The
horror is in the architecture around them, in the fact that the form will never be
approved, and in the knowledge that this same person will describe the encounter
quite differently to a colleague this afternoon.

=== THE PEOPLE ===
Groomed, calm, expensively dressed, unhurried. Tailored coats, clean lines, soft
neutrals with one blue accent. Visible chrome is a STATUS SYMBOL, not a disfigurement:
a polished temple plate worn the way somebody wears a watch, a hand that is obviously
manufactured and obviously the best one money can buy. The further up the ladder, the
less flesh, and the more serene.

At the very top: a person mostly made of machine, beautifully finished, pleasant to
talk to, entirely unreachable.

Nobody looks like a soldier. Everybody looks like they have an appointment.

=== TONE ===
Clean, bright, generous, total. Not oppressive, not dystopian-grim, not cold-war
brutalist. Closer to a flagship tech store, a first-class terminal, a private
hospital, a corporate campus that genuinely is a nice place to work. The horror is
that it is all true and it all works.

=== THE ONE IMAGE THAT SAYS IT ===
A white, sunlit clinic atrium under a curtain-wall ribbon of glass, one blue lit line
running the length of a seamless counter. A person sits calmly while a machine that
makes no noise replaces their arm. Behind them, a wall of small etched numbers: every
procedure performed in the building this year, and a number for the person in the
chair.
```
