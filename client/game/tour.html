Everything is simulated

The city doesn't wait for you to log in

A day passes every eight real hours. Weather rolls across the map. Streetlights come on at dusk because a power grid decided they should. Shopkeepers walk home and go to bed. None of it is on a script that starts when you arrive.

⚡A real power grid

Generators, substations, fuses. Storms fault them. Blackouts are simulated, not announced — the lights just go out, and the ATM stops working.

👃Smell and sound

Rooms have odours and noises that travel. A gunshot next door is something you hear. So is somebody who hasn't washed.

🧠NPCs who remember

Familiarity and warmth, tracked per person. The barkeep stops greeting you like a stranger. Some doors only open for someone known.

🍳Cooking, simulated properly

The pan tracks its own contents, heat and doneness. Chop, season, stir, flip, taste — timing is a real variable, and a dish you invent is yours to name and sell.

🩹Gear that wears out

Condition degrades with use, not with the clock. Repair it well and it comes back tougher. Ignore it and it's destroyed.

🧹Mess that persists

Unowned rooms get swept nightly. A room you rent keeps its stains for a full rent cycle. That's why you'd carry a mop.

The storyline you argue with

Nine orders. Two questions. Pick a side or don't.

Something much smarter than people built this place and may still be running it. Everyone in Coldwater Basin has a theory about what to do next, and the theories don't get along. Standing with an order is something you keep being — stop showing up and you drift back to being nobody in particular. Four of the nine recruit, trade and hold grudges today; the other five have creeds and a seat on the chart, and you can read them but not yet join them.

Become something

Mutant, machine, or master of the trade

Three ways to stop being an ordinary body, and the first two are a genuine fork in the road.

☢Mutate

Radiation is patient. Let it build and it rewrites you — permanent mutations with real benefits and real costs. Free, random, and not your decision once you've let it get that far.

⚙Get chromed

Cybernetics are the opposite: chosen, paid for, slot-limited, removable. Subdermal weave is armour under the skin. A cortical backup means dying is a rollback.

✦Master a craft

26 skills, no experience bar, no level gates. You get better at picking locks by picking locks. You become whatever you keep doing.

You cannot be both. Your first cybernetic install burns off every mutation you have — and chrome can't mutate afterward. Flesh and machine are a door that locks behind you, and the human-path orders will hold it against you.

— the flesh / machine fork

And then there's a flight simulator in it

Aircraft you actually fly

Not a travel menu with a loading bar. A continuous energy simulation with stalls that have teeth, per-airframe fuel, weather you can see coming, and a cockpit full of instruments that mean something. Take off, get lost, run cargo, get shot at.

  • Cargo, medevac, VIP charter, survey, relief — and the contracts that don't file a flight plan.
  • Air-to-air and air-to-ground — guns, missiles, SAM sites that resent you.
  • A licence you have to earn, a hangar to keep your fleet in, and insurance for when you don't.
  • Break-up and crash cameras, because the game wants to show you exactly how it ended.

Things to do that aren't violence

Sit down and play something

Real games, fully implemented, with other people at the table.

♟Chess

Complete legal rules — castling, en passant, promotion, repetition — verified against known move counts. Rendered as an isometric neon set where the pieces stand up off the tilted board.

🂡Poker

Seats, blinds, a host who deals, spectators behind you. Play it on the felt, or have the whole table called out to you in words.

🎰The casino

Slots and a poker room at The Lucky Bastard, for when you've decided money is a burden.

🃏Trading cards

Mint yourself at a terminal and become a card someone else can pull out of a foil sleeve. Packs roll when you open them, never when you buy them.

🎣Fishing & mining

Cast and wait, or work a deposit. Both are postures: start them and they keep going while you watch the water.

🎹Instruments

Sit at the upright and play it, key by key, on your own keyboard. The room hears what you play. No skill check — the skill is your hands.

Make a living, or make a problem

From counting rats to running the block

Honest work

A rotating job board of small legal gigs — deliver a parcel, sweep a lot, count the rats, sit in a seat somebody needs filled. Steady shifts. Fishing and scavenging for what you can carry.

Your own shop

Buy the deed to a storefront and it's yours: set the wares, order stock, hire and sack staff, work the till, pull the shutters at night. Rename it something worse.

Your own flat

Rent a unit, lock it, upgrade the lock before somebody picks it. Sleep safe, store your things, and deal with your own mess.

Or run a corporation

A corp is a faction with an owner, a treasury, members and territory — and territory is a tug-of-war you can lose. Five levers of influence, and a war phase for when influence stops being enough.

Protection rackets. Control a zone and you can shake down the NPC shops in it for a cut of the take. Every shop remembers how afraid it is of you — and the racket fights the safecrackers over the same pot.

— corps, lever E

The other economy

A whole chemistry set, and a market for it

Taking them

Timed effects that ramp and fade. Addiction rolls, tolerance, withdrawal, overdose. Drinking a beer takes twelve to eighteen seconds and walking out of the room interrupts it — nothing here is a button you mash.

Some of it takes you out of your body. The dissociatives don't decorate the room, they remove you from it: your mind walks a handful of rooms that assemble themselves for the trip and dissolve when it ends, while your body stays exactly where you dropped it — in a real room, lootable and killable by anyone who wanders past.

Making and moving them

Synthesis and splicing on a real bench, behind a cabinet built to not look like a lab. Raw supply chains. Dealers to sell through, and a street price that isn't fixed.

All of it is witnessed by somebody eventually. The city runs a wanted level, police who will take you alive at the top of it, a jail, an evidence locker, and a cell door that can be hacked.

Weather that is trying to kill you

Get cold. Get wet. Get dissolved.

Severity is a live number, not a backdrop, and it is gear-gated lethal: weather will kill you, but only if you went out unprepared. The right coat turns a killer into an inconvenience, and that trade is what the whole system is built on.

🥶Hypothermia

Body temperature is tracked. Soaked clothing pulls heat out of you faster. Fall in the water and get out slower than you went in.

🌧Soaked through

Rain wets your clothes layer by layer, and wet layers stop working. Dry off or keep paying for it.

🧪Acid rain

A named hero event. It eats gear and skin. Being indoors helps; being indoors without a roof over the right room does not.

💨Wind

Never blocks you — just charges you stamina for every step outdoors until you're too tired to be somewhere dangerous.

⚡Ion storm

An EMP that drops the whole city grid. Lights, ATMs, televisions, cyberware. The aftermath persists.

⚠No safe haven

A blacked-out building has no heating. Indoors isn't a solved problem, just a different one.

There is television, and it is real television

Shows assembled live, performed by actual NPCs

Every set in the world is furniture on the power grid. Tune it and what comes out is not a canned string. It's a show built fresh from what's happening in the city that day, acted by people who had to turn up to work.

🎙The talk show

A host and a sidekick who commute in on schedule. A guest NPC renamed to a new persona each day, who appears somewhere in the world and walks across the map to the studio — and if they don't make it, there's an authored no-show segment instead of an interview with furniture.

📰News, with a weather desk

Stories sourced from real events in the world, padded out with wire copy and tabloid filler, read by fixed outlet bylines. The meteorologist works off the same seven-day forecast you do.

🏆Two live sports

Deadball on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. Cluster Puck on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays. Real leagues, real standings, a postseason, a score bug, and a bookmaker who'll take your money on it.

🎬And a game show you can be on

Stand in the studio when a round opens and you're a contestant — answering on air, citywide, for money. One asks what things are worth. The other asks what you know about the city.

There are also morning shows, sermons, films that play in real time so you can join one already running, pirate broadcasts, and a bystander line that leaks in when somebody else is watching a set in the same room as you.

The part that matters most

The world lives on without you

Log out for a week. The city does not pause, wait, or reset.

  • Your rent falls due on the city calendar whether you're there to pay it or not.
  • Your hunger, thirst and radiation keep moving. You come back to a body that's been somewhere.
  • Weather rolls on — the storm you were warned about happens on schedule, with you or without.
  • NPCs work their shifts, commute home, go to bed, and slowly forget who you were.
  • Leagues play their fixtures and the standings move without a single player watching.
  • Your corpse is still lying there if that's how you left. Somebody has probably been through it.

A world that only happens while you're looking at it is a ride. This one is a place you visit.
