Coldwater Basin The lights still work Nobody asks why

Humanity expected extinction.It got a commute.

The war took about three weeks. When the sky finally cleared and people came up expecting ash, what they found instead was a city with the lights on and traffic backing up at the lights. Nobody rebuilt Coldwater. It just never stopped.

Something keeps it running and has never explained itself. The shelves fill overnight. The buses are roughly on time. After a couple of generations most people have stopped asking about it, because there's rent to make and the rent doesn't care.

"It did not save humanity. It kept a habit going. Nobody has been able to say whose." From the Codex

What we actually built

A city that gets on with its day whether or not you're logged in. The people who live here hold jobs and walk home from them. They drink, sleep badly, hold grudges, and after you've been in a few times they start recognising you — which cuts both ways, because they remember the last conversation as clearly as you do.

Nearly everything you'd expect to be scenery turns out to be running underneath. Weather that ruins the coat you're standing in. A coat that wears through where it's been hit, and a man across town who can make it better than new if you're on decent terms with him. Food that comes out well or badly depending on whether you were paying attention. Hunger, thirst, cold, sleep, the slow cost of a bad habit. None of it is a number on a screen you can ignore — it all talks to everything else, and it carries on doing so while you're away.

We didn't build a story for you to walk through. We built somewhere to live, and left the consequences switched on.

And it moves when you push it

You arrive with no history and nobody waiting on you, which is the good news. Everything after that is yours. Rent a room, then a floor, then a shop with your name over the door. Learn to fly and take work nobody else will. Start a company, take a district off somebody who had it first. Get known as a cook, a pilot, a fence, or a problem — and find out that being known is a thing with a price attached.

Or don't. Get a job, keep your head down, make friends with the neighbours and pay your rent on time under streetlights that have never once gone off. The city will let you. It's been letting people do that for years, and it has never told any of them why.
