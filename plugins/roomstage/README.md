# roomstage

**Purpose:** walk round a flat in first person. `room` opens the Room Stage in any flat (`flags.is_apartment`, let or vacant), or in any room carrying `flags.room`, and from that flat's en-suite; the tenant and visitors alike can open it, and everyone in the room sees everyone else. An `is_dwelling` zone such as the Solenne Penthouse isn't a flat, so it needs its own `flags.room`. It's the shared layer the Platinum Box suite walks on too ([docs/proposals/room-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/room-stage.md), [docs/proposals/home-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/home-stage.md)).

## Commands
- `room`: open the room in 3-D. Refused where there's no room to walk round, and at the log display rung.
- `room close`: shut it. Walking out of the room shuts it too.
- `arrange <piece> <wall>`: move one of the room's player pieces against another wall: `left`, `right`, `window` (or `front`) or `door` (or `back`). `arrange <piece>` with no wall puts it back where `place` would set it now. Anyone who controls the room may (its tenant, a corp's HQ manager, a shop's owner: furnishing's `place` rule); an authored piece refuses ("It's fixed in place."), and so does a piece somebody is sitting or lying on. Works at every display rung: the room text doesn't change, only `flags.spot`.

## The payload
`room_view` is the whole room: `spec` (`{ w, d, h, unit, door, window, windows, finish, plan?, balcony?, annex?, pit?, ground?, openings?, windowless? }` in metres), `building?` (`{ id, name }` on a building's walk), `via?` (the room you just walked in from), `here` (the zone you're in, the flat or its en-suite), `hasView` (a `room_outlook` is on its way), `pieces` (`{ id, name, arche, form, dims, spot, wear, seed, verbs, container, annex? }`), `people` (`{ id, name, posture, on, at?, yaw?, seat?, npc?, order?, annex? }`, where `order` is an NPC's order id when it has one, which the client dresses as that order dresses: a Wildblood in the tunic and mantle, docs/proposals/hd-figure.md), `beasts` (`{ id, name, fauna, annex? }`: the enemies in the room whose `flags.fauna` names a fauna model, drawn by `gl/room-beasts.js` from its close-up body in the beast kit (`gl/beast-looks.js`; the gutter hound has one) or else from the model GLASS uses; an enemy with no model is left off the floor, and a 2 s RAM sweep sends `room_people` again when one comes, goes or dies), `mine` (whether you control the room) and `clock` (the server's fractional hour, for the light through the window). After it, `room_outlook` sends the world round the building for the view out (`{ gx, gy, storey, storeys, bearing, map, skyline, sky, roads, regions, fields, contacts, actors }`, the freelook window, with the airfields flights go between and what's moving), `room_ahead` the rooms one doorway away as each would open (`{ zone, rooms: [{ zone, spec, lift?, neighbours }] }`, for the client to build before you walk there; [building-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/building-stage.md), "Baked ahead"), `room_piece` one piece placed or taken (or, with `pieces`, the whole room after a move-out), `room_people` who's there now, `room_here` which side of the en-suite's door you're on and `room_sky` the weather at the tile, what's moving and who's on the pavement as they change (`{ zone, contacts, actors?, weather?, wind?, windDir?, here?, event?, field? }`).

`room_avail` (`{ on }`) tells the d-pad's room button whether the room you're in has a Stage. It goes at login and when the answer changes, so a walk down a street sends nothing. `room_view` and `room_close` turn the button solid and back. The Stage covers the d-pad, so its lit Close button is the way off.

A room that answers the sync `room.vehicles` gather carries `vehicles` on `room_view`, and `room.vehiclesChanged` sends it again to each viewer as `room_vehicles` (gathered per viewer). Only a truck depot's shed answers: the truck on its lift and the viewer's trucks downstairs (plugins/trucking `hoist.js`, docs/systems-trucking.md "The lift in the shed"); the client draws it with `client/game/js/panels/room-hoist.js`.

A room with a tag on its own wall (or its en-suite's) carries `tags` on `room_view`, from the sync `room.tags` gather (plugins/graffiti): `[{ zone, t, r?, f?, spot?, v }]`, the words as typed, their runs and letterform, and `spot` (`{ u, v, y, n }`, metres in the Stage's frame) where the can was aimed. `graffiti.tagged` and `graffiti.removed` send the whole list again as `room_tags` to whoever has the room open. The client draws them on the plaster (`client/game/js/panels/room-tags.js`, `setDecals` in `gl/room.js`). G, or the can in Pockets (`act: 'spray'`, from `pocketsOf`), opens the can with `spraycan`, and the spray session runs over the Stage (plugins/graffiti README, "In 3-D: the spray session").

## Events
- Consumes `graffiti.tagged` and `graffiti.removed` (`{ zoneId, targetZoneId? }`, plugins/graffiti): a tag went up on a room's wall or came off it.
- Consumes `room.vehiclesChanged` (`{ zoneId }`), emitted by trucking when a truck comes up on a shed's lift, goes down or is driven off it.
- Consumes `furniture.changed` (`{ zoneId, id, gone }`), emitted by the furnishing plugin's `place`, `take` and move-out boxing, by a vendor's delivery in `server/engine/furniture-shop.js` and by this plugin's own `arrange`.
- Consumes `room.pos` (`{ playerId, at, yaw, seat }`), which `server/index.js` emits for a client's `room_pos` message.
- Consumes `room.act` (`{ playerId, act, hold }`) for a client's `room_act`, each beat of a first-person ritual, and `player.drugUsed` for a dose taken any other way (`acts.js`). A player in `room_people` then carries `act`, `hold` and `beat` until 12 s pass without a beat: the client plays the act's clip with the thing in their hand ([room-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/room-stage.md), "Eating, drinking and taking something"). RAM only; nothing here doses anybody.
- Consumes `room.pockets` (`{ playerId }`) for a client's `room_pockets`, and answers `room_pockets` (`{ items: [{ id, item, name, qty, act, hold, verb }] }`): what they carry that the room can take in first person, by one query on the click (`pocketsOf`).
- Consumes `zone.entered`, `posture.changed` and `player.logout` to keep the people current.

## Rules worth knowing
- **The Stage decides nothing.** Every gesture sends a verb a player could type: `sit <piece>`, `lie <piece>`, `stand`, `open <piece>`, `watch <piece>`, `examine <piece>`. The room text and the text rungs lose nothing, and a posture typed in the log moves you on the Stage, because your own posture comes back in `room_people`.
- **A piece's look comes from data.** `archetypeOf`, `formOf` and `dimsOf` in `client/shared/room-layout.js` read the row's type, its flags and its weight (a deep-freeze is a chest, a heavy lie-only piece is a pod, `flags.wardrobe` is a wardrobe); `flags.look` (`{ arche, form, dims, wear }`) overrides any of them. Nothing reads an item's name, so a row whose flags can't say what it is (a mattress and a sofa are both `sit` and `lie`) carries a `flags.look`, which `scripts/content/room-looks.mjs` writes for the flats.
- **A flat is a studio.** A room of 6.5 × 7.5 m or more has a `plan`: a clear lobby at the door, a kitchen down the right-hand wall behind an L of counter, the bed in the back left and the lounge out front. `placeSpot` puts a piece in its own part of the room by its archetype, and the counter and wall units are part of the room, not rows. See [docs/proposals/room-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/room-stage.md#the-studio-plan).
- **A floor can be sunk.** `flags.room.pit` (`{ at: [u0, u1, v0, v1], depth, step }`, in metres) lets a rectangle into the floor with a ledge round it at half the depth, so you walk down into it anywhere. A piece whose middle is on the pit's floor stands on it: `roomLayout` lowers its seats, stations and climb (`y0`) and `K.lift` draws it down there, so no form has to know. `L.groundAt` is where a walker stands and `L.baseAt` where a figure's feet go. `placeSpot` keeps out of a pit, so what's in one has a `flags.spot`. The Solenne Penthouse has the one there is, with `flags.room.ground: 'stone'`, which lays the room in `roomStone` instead of its finish's boards. A pit can be round instead: `{ round: [u, v, r], depth, step }`, as the Cherry Pit's is, with a chrome rail round its rim open toward the back wall.
- **Dancers work the poles.** An NPC with `flags.stripper` on shift in their work zone takes the next free pole (the runway's tip first, then the stage's) and is sent with `at`, `face` and `lift` (the piece's top), idling on the `pole` clips; with no pole free they work the floor (`floorwork`). While any NPC is at work, `flags.figure_work` is laid over `flags.figure`, so a dancer arrives in her street clothes and changes for the shift.
- **A working building dresses from its decor.** `flags.room.decor` can carry `strips` (ceiling lights, which then light the room instead of a pendant shade), `pipes`, `drops` (cable bundles down a wall), `paint` (hazard bands, lines with arrows, outlines), `grates`, `screens` (`vitals`, `ad`, `gauges`, `status`), `signs`, `pumps` and `lamps`, all placed by the room's file and drawn by `facility` in `gl/room-decor.js`. `flags.room.ground` can be `tile` or `concrete` as well as `stone`, `marble` and `rubber`. The clone facility's two floors are the example: a print vat is the `vat` form, its plate lettered from `look.tag`, and `look.state: 'drained'` is the open one you came out of.
- **A club has more than a flat.** `flags.room.walls` stands partitions in the room (`[[u0, u1, v0, v1], ...]`, full height, solid to a walker); `flags.room.windowless` drops the window; `flags.room.doors.down` (`[u, v]`, with `run`) draws a stair well going down from that spot toward the left-hand wall, and `building-graph.js` stacks the room below so its own `doors.up` lands under it. The `stage` and `runway` forms (`surface`) stand at floor level even over a pit, and the `banquette` form (`bench`) is a ring of seats round a round pit, every seat facing in and looking up.
- **A balcony is part of the walk.** A studio above the ground floor has one, reached by a door in the lounge. Stepping out sends nothing, because it isn't a zone. `room.pos` clamps to it.
- **A set shows the broadcast.** `watch` and `tune` work as they always have; on the client the answer goes to the set on the wall (`client/game/js/panels/room-tv.js`). This plugin takes no part in it. Each viewer watches for themselves.
- **What's drawn is `rowDrawn`.** Furniture, containers, beds, sets, plumbing and media decks, and a fixture with a stove, a microwave, a kettle or water. A shelf of a cabinet (`compartment_of`) is part of the cabinet. A concealed piece stays out of the payload until it's found, since the payload is the room and the hover says its name.
- **A flat and its en-suite are one Stage.** The zone `in` reaches, whose `out` comes back and whose parent is the flat, is drawn behind the back wall with its door open. Everything is keyed on the flat (`mainOf`), `room` opens from either, and a piece in the en-suite carries `annex` and a spot in the en-suite's own frame. Walking through the door sends the ordinary `in` or `out`; `zone.entered` keeps the Stage open and answers `room_here`. If it refuses, nothing answers, and the client puts you back.
- **A building's rooms are one walk.** In a building whose entry has a Stage (a `maps` row's `entry_zone_id`), the rooms with a Stage reached from it are laid out as one plan (`buildingOfRoom`, client/shared/building-graph.js), and `room_view` carries each room's doorways as `spec.openings` and `building` (`{ id, name, neighbours }`, the rooms one open doorway away, each placed from this one, with its `pieces` and `people`), both from `buildingView`. Somebody with a room open is sent the `room_people` and `room_piece` of the rooms next door as they change (`watchersOf`), and every layout of a building room's pieces reads `stageSpec`, the spec with its doorways, so nothing laid out by the rules stands in one. A doorway wins over a window in the same wall. Walking through one sends the ordinary `move <dir>`; `zone.entered` sends the next room's `room_view` with `via`, the room you came from, instead of `room_close`. If the move's refused, nothing answers, and the client puts you back inside the doorway. The plans are built from RAM when a room opens and again when the count of zones or maps changes, never on a move ([docs/proposals/building-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/building-stage.md)).
- **The view out is the world.** `room_outlook` gives the tile, how far up the building you are (`flags.room.storey`, else the number on the zone's `marker`) and the way the front wall faces (`flags.room.face`, else away from the way in). `client/game/js/panels/room-outlook.js` runs GLASS from there, under the room. A room with no tile on the map has no outlook and keeps the painted street, and so does a phone.
- **An authored row may fix its own spot** with `flags.spot`, in the same shape as a stamped one. Without it, it's worked out by the same rules every time, what needs a wall first (`placeRank`).
- **A kettle or a microwave goes on a worktop.** An authored one gets `{ on, x }`, a place on the sink's run or a counter already in the room. A player's piece doesn't, since the worktop can be taken away from under it; a player's microwave stands on its trolley.
- **A player's spot is stamped once, then moved only by `arrange`.** `flags.spot` (`{ wall, along, turn }` or `{ at: [u, v], turn }`, in metres) is written to the row when it lands. `arrange` takes the first free candidate on the named wall from `placeSpot` (its `wall` option) run over the room without the piece, so the way in from the door, the window and the overlap test still decide; a full wall refuses. It's one `updateFurniture` write, then `furniture.changed`, so everyone with the Stage open gets `room_piece` and sees the piece move. An authored piece is never written to, since git is the only writer of content; its spot is worked out by the same rules every time, before any player piece, so it doesn't move either.
- **Positions are RAM.** The client sends `room_pos` when you stop walking or sit, not every step, throttled to one per 150 ms per player. A restart forgets them and everyone starts by the door.
- **The view out is GLASS, live, under the room.** The panes are holes in the room's canvas. Scheduled flights are a pure function of the wall clock (`client/shared/window-sky.js`) and go to GLASS as contacts. See [docs/proposals/room-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/room-stage.md#the-outlook).
- **The lights are the viewer's.** The switch and the dimmer are per viewer, in `localStorage`, and send nothing. A fire isn't on them.
- **One 5 s tick, and no hot-path queries.** `skyTick` is one `Map.size` when no room is open, and it reads RAM when one is. `zone.entered` and `posture.changed` return on one `Map.get` unless someone has that room open. The only writes are the spot stamps and `arrange`'s one write. `room_outlook` is the freelook payload, sent once per open and never on a tick.

## A Cachet in a room

A disguise (`flags.conceal_hides`) is a piece with `keypad`, and what it hides never is one: it's the back of the same turntable, form `chemwall`, with `turned` naming it once it's open. The payload says nothing about it while sealed. `keypad` on `room_view` says this viewer gets the advert (concealment's rule); anyone else's click examines it. The code goes through the ordinary keypad overlay. Opening or sealing emits `furniture.changed`, and the client turns the piece half round on the moving layer (`spinBatch`), then rebuilds. The bench's station sends `workspace`, whose panel comes up over the room. A chem lab that isn't behind a Cachet is the same `chemwall` piece with the same `workspace` station, and either one is drawn at its `station_quality` grade (`lab` on the piece, `gl/lab-mesh.js`; docs/proposals/chem-stage.md). A lab's station sends `workspace chembench`, so a stove in the same room doesn't take it, and on the visual rung the bench comes up in 3D at the lab itself: the room hosts the Chem Stage (`chem:open`), drawing it from its camera with its props, liquids, halos and glows. A Cachet authored wider than its 2.2 m turntable (or its `look.open`) stands in a false wall with an alcove behind (`cachetFrame`), and instead of turning it assembles: the bar wall parts and goes into the wall, and the lab comes together part by part (`assembly` in gl/room-mesh.js).

## People at home

A person in `room_people` carries their `sex`, and an NPC with a settled look its `flags.figure`, which the sculpted close-up figure draws them by ([sculpted-figure.md](../../docs/proposals/sculpted-figure.md)).

An NPC the engine has asleep carries `asleep`, and one out cold `out`, as the room text tags them. If nothing has put them on a piece, `peopleIn` lays them on a bed, else a sofa, else sits them in a chair, one body a piece; before this they stood by the door. `manner` is what they idle at, off `flags.personality`: `bar` and `shop` only while `isNpcAtWork`, `guard` and `rough` always. All of it is read from RAM.

A resident with a habit (`flags.drug_habit`, `preshow_habit` or `booze_habit`) now and then sits down at a table in their room to it: `stash` on the person (`{ k, of, beat, gone, at }`, client/shared/table-stash.js) is the kit, how many, how long one takes and the spot on the top. The client works the clip out from that clock (`sitCut` then `sitLine` for a line, `sitPill`, `sitPipe`, `sitTie`, `sitHuff`, `sitDrink`) and `gl/room-stash.js` draws the kit going down one at a time, so nothing is sent per beat. An npc-drugs ritual beat that lands on somebody in a chair plays the chair's version (`SIT_TAKE`) rather than standing them up. It's the picture's, like a meal: nothing here doses anybody.

`npc-life.js` walks residents round their flat while its Stage is open: RAM only, routes sent in `room_people`, the NPC's real posture untouched. They also stand about on the phone, smoke at the window or wait (`clip` on a person). A lie seat and a sit seat on one piece can't both be used (`seatsClash`). Someone who sits facing a set turns it on (`tv` on `room_view` and `room_people`, and the client sends `watch`) and off when they get up. A resident in the en-suite carries `shut`, which closes its door until you walk up to it. A banter scene turns its two to face each other (`npc.banter.start` and `.end`); the words stay the engine's, so the text world reads them, and on the Stage a line goes in a bubble over the speaker's head if they're in view. See "People at home" in [room-stage.md](../../docs/proposals/room-stage.md).
