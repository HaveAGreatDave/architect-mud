CPhL — FIGHT VIEW / FIGURE ART TEST

Onlookers removed. Figures rebuilt with hockey gear proper — shoulder pads, elbow pads, fat pants with a side stripe, banded socks, boot-and-blade skates, heads in profile. And it now actually animates: a 60fps rAF loop tweening between keyframe poses with per-beat easing, screen shake, hit-stop and follow-through — not a slideshow.

THE SOUNDSET (FM · client/shared/hockey-sfx.js)

Built on the engine that's already here — these are data in the same layer schema sfx-catalog.js uses, so they drop into that catalog and become dev-panel editable. The engine needed exactly one new thing: a modulation-index envelope. Click anything to audition; every press varies slightly, on purpose.

NHL ’96 MODE (vertical · following camera · high-angle)

Same sim, second renderer. The point isn't the orientation — it's that the camera only shows about a third of the ice, so you get six big players instead of twelve tiny ones. High angle also means figures just rotate, which dissolves the facing problem the side-on view has to fake with squash. Try ◧ RETRO PALETTE with this one.

THE RINK (side-on — moving puck · steered skaters · beacon)

3/4 perspective, the way the arcade games did it — which also means the side-on figure renderer gets reused at small scale instead of needing separate top-down art. The puck follows a Catmull-Rom spline through eight sparse keyframes (the ~600 bytes the server would actually send); everyone else is improvised by steering toward role anchors relative to the puck. Press PLAY. It scores every ~7s.

THE FIGHT (side-on — the authentic hockey framing)

Press ▶ PLAY FIGHT. Watch for: the wind-up easing in slowly then the punch snapping out in ~110ms, the hit-stop freeze on contact, the screen shake, the head snapping back with overshoot and settling. It ends with the loser in the box and a power play — the fight moving the scoreboard.

C — VARIATION: TWELVE SEEDS

If these read as one guy in twelve jerseys, procedural figures are wrong regardless of how any single pose looks.

D — THE CHEAP FALLBACK

Cartoon scuffle cloud. ~20 lines, always shippable. The bar the figures have to clear.

How to judge.
  • Play it before anything else. Static poses flatter; motion is where cheap art dies.
  • Does it read as a broadcast cutting to a fight, at a glance, with no explanation?
  • In C, are those twelve different people?
  • Is it honestly better than D? If not, D wins — it was always a good answer.
